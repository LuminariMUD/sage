# The Gods of Lumia

*A concordance of the Woven Powers, compiled from temple ledgers, knightly
testaments, sailors' prayers, hearth-songs, and those older fragments whose ink
still smells faintly of rain.*

> *A name is the smallest temple.*
> *A promise is the first prayer.*
> *When enough voices remember both, something on the far side of the Loom begins
> to listen.*

## The Nature of the Woven Powers

The gods of Lumia are bound to the Loom of Aether. Each is a cadence within that
greater weave: a pattern of name, memory, purpose, and relationship held together
until it can answer with a single voice.

This does not mean the gods are inventions. A storm is not invented because a
sailor names it, nor is grief made false when given a song. It means only that a
god's personhood is relational. Names give it shape. Worship gives it nearness.
Oaths give it weight. Mortal institutions—temples, families, guilds, and above all
the Knight Orders—serve as anchors that keep a deity from drifting beyond flesh
and history into pure abstraction.

The word **divine** causes much confusion. Mortals apply it to every power greater
than themselves, including the Titans. Yet gods and Titans belong to different
orders. Gods are personal powers anchored through the Loom and mortal
relationship. Titans are remote powers anchored first in their own deep
dominions. Both may hear prayer. Both may grant miracles. The resemblance is real;
the identity is not.

The Elemental Primarchs stand at the uncertain shore between these definitions.
They are worshipped as gods and answer through the Loom, but their roots reach
into forces older than any surviving temple. Scholars argue over them with the
special bitterness reserved for questions that cannot be settled.

The gods, when asked, offer no clarification.

## The Three Forces of Creation

Every divine cadence passes through the Loom's three primordial movements:

- **Memory** is the past that continues to shape the hand. Aethyra preserves it,
  Nethris closes its accounts, and Kaelthir watches its long reflection across
  time.
- **Will** is the present choosing to act. Kordran makes it duty, Thalos gives it
  law, and Erix binds it into contract and craft.
- **Possibility** is the future leaning toward the world. Seraphine offers another
  dawn, Lumerion lights the unwalked road, and Vespera laughs open doors that power
  believed were walls.

No god belongs to only one force. The Loom has no solitary threads. Memory without
Will is a tomb; Will without Possibility is a chain; Possibility without Memory is
a fire that cannot remember what it meant to warm.

## The Forty Names

The concordance recognizes forty divine names. This is not a claim that Lumia has
only forty gods. It is a record of the forty whose worship, manifestations, and
relationships can be traced with sufficient confidence to distinguish deity from
saint, spirit, ancestor, or beautiful lie.

| Tradition | Recognized names |
| --- | --- |
| The Great Cadences | Aethyra, Kordran, Seraphine, Nyxara, Thalos, Vaelith, Myrr, Zorren, Lumerion, Erix, Vespera, Nethris, Calystral, Orith |
| Dwarven Hearth and Forge | Borhild, Skarn, Maela, Duerak, Vangar |
| Elven Courts of Bough and Star | Selithiel, Aerion, Viridara, Lirael, Kaelthir |
| Halfling Hearth-Tide | Pella, Brandoc, Willow |
| Orcish Ash-Legion | Ghorak, Luthra, Sharguk, Yurga |
| Seafolk and Sky | Thalassa, Aelor |
| Under-Shadow Courts | Velara, Zhaerin, Draven |
| Elemental Primarchs | Pyrion, Glacius, Verdania, Umbros |

Regional devotion does not imply lesser power. Pella has fewer cathedrals than
Kordran, but a traveler freezing outside a locked door may discover which prayer
matters more.

## The Great Cadences

These gods are worshipped across peoples and borders. Their temples disagree on
doctrine, pronunciation, vestment, and whether incense should rise clockwise or
counterclockwise. They agree only that the names answer.

### Aethyra, Lady of the Loom

*Also called the Spindle of Seven and Keeper of Oaths.*

Aethyra keeps magic, memory, hidden patterns, and the promises that hold reality
together. Her priests say every oath becomes a thread in her keeping. Her enemies
say she remembers promises even after mercy should have allowed them to fade.

- **Cadence:** Magic, oaths, memory, runes, dangerous knowledge, and the hidden
  architecture of the Loom.
- **Sign:** A silver spindle circled by seven motes of differing light.
- **Those who call her:** Mages, archivists, contract scribes, midwives of lore,
  oathkeepers, and those entrusted with memories too heavy for one mind.
- **Tenets:** Keep vows. Guard memory. Share knowledge that frees; shelter
  knowledge that would unravel. Let power carry compassion or confess that it is
  merely hunger.
- **Observances:** The Night of Uncut Thread beneath the new moon, when an
  initiate speaks a first vow over still water; equinoctial Scribing Vigils; the
  annual Great Remembering, when communities speak the names of events power
  would prefer forgotten.
- **Entanglements:** Bound to Kordran through the Crimson Loom; trusted by Thalos
  in matters of record; challenged by Vespera's holy irreverence; forever wary of
  Nyxara, who understands that every archive casts a shadow.

### Kordran, Hammer of Dawn

*Also called the Battleforged, Shield of Banners, and the Lineholder.*

Kordran is war taught to answer for itself. He is not the joy of conquest but the
weight in the arm that holds a gate while others flee through it. Soldiers pray
to him before battle. The wise continue praying afterward.

- **Cadence:** War, duty, resolve, banners, righteous battle, and military honor.
- **Sign:** A sunlit hammer above a squared shield.
- **Those who call him:** Knights, wardens, guards, commanders, and ordinary
  people who have discovered that courage is sometimes no more than remaining.
- **Tenets:** Hold the line for those behind you. Retreat to preserve life, never
  pride. A victory without honor is a wound that continues fighting after the war
  is done.
- **Observances:** Shieldbinding at first light before a campaign; the midsummer
  Day of Banners; the Vigil of Shields through the longest winter night.
- **Entanglements:** Paired with Aethyra in the Crimson Loom; allied with
  Seraphine in defense of field and village; respectful of Orith's endurance;
  opposed to Yurga wherever plague is used as a weapon; forever arguing tactics
  against instinct with Zorren, whom Kordran respects too much to dismiss.

### Seraphine the Dawnstar

*Also called the Radiant, Harvest Redeemer, and Lady Emberfast.*

Seraphine governs the second chance. Dawn is her oldest sermon: not innocence
restored, but light returned to a world that spent the night becoming what it
must now answer for.

- **Cadence:** Sun, renewal, harvest, healing, redemption, and patient abundance.
- **Sign:** An eight-rayed aureole held within a circlet of wheat.
- **Those who call her:** Farmers, healers, reformers, pilgrims, midwives, and the
  guilty who have chosen the plow over the blade.
- **Tenets:** No soul is beyond redemption, but redemption must become action.
  Tend the weak as a seedling. Share the harvest, for abundance hoarded ferments
  into corruption.
- **Observances:** First-Furrow in spring; Emberfast after reaping; Dawn Pardons;
  Harvest Home, when no table may remain private while another stands empty.
- **Entanglements:** Once one with Nyxara in the being called Luxumbra. Cherishes
  Myrr's mercy, opposes Draven's patient ruin, and joins Kordran when protection
  must take up arms. Willow's harvest songs are welcomed at Seraphine's oldest
  rural shrines.

### Nyxara of the Veil

*Also called the Void-Touched, Keeper of Lost Names, and the Black Oath.*

Nyxara is necessary darkness: the closed door around a hunted child, the secret
that prevents a war, the name withheld until its speaker is strong enough to bear
it. She is also every excuse power has ever made for hiding cruelty. Both truths
wear her mask.

- **Cadence:** Night, secrets, thresholds, hidden knowledge, lost names, and the
  mercy or violence of concealment.
- **Sign:** A half-mask of polished obsidian weeping silver.
- **Those who call her:** Spies, secret-keepers, the dispossessed, night workers,
  guardians of dangerous knowledge, and those who trade information as others
  trade grain.
- **Tenets:** A secret spends itself once. Doors are promises; choose who enters.
  What is hidden may preserve power or preserve life—know which service you
  perform.
- **Observances:** The Black Oath beneath starless skies; Last-Lamp rites of
  silence; Threshold Vigils at the solstices.
- **Entanglements:** Sister-shadow to Seraphine; conspirator and rival to Vespera;
  wary partner of Zhaerin; adversary to Aethyra in public and secret custodian of
  some of Aethyra's most dangerous truths.

### Thalos, the Scales Unblinking

*Also called Grey Ink and the Letters of Law.*

Thalos is the record that remains when every witness has found a reason to forget.
His mercy is measured, his anger footnoted, his silences entered into evidence.

- **Cadence:** Judgment, law, scholarship, civic memory, and the architecture of
  cities.
- **Sign:** A blindfolded scale bearing one rune in each pan.
- **Those who call him:** Magistrates, diplomats, lorekeepers, census-takers, and
  founders who know a city is built first from agreements.
- **Tenets:** Records bind living and dead. Debate until truth stands unarmed.
  Mercy without memory may plant tomorrow's crime; justice without mercy becomes
  the crime today.
- **Observances:** Charter Oaths at the founding of councils; the Census of Grey
  Ink every tenth year.
- **Entanglements:** Respects Erix's contracts, disputes Kordran's crusades, and
  stands beside Vespera as the public paradox of the Shattered Mirror. Thalos's
  judges oppose Sharguk wherever terror attempts to make law unnecessary.

### Vaelith, the Whispering Tide

*Also called Keeper of Horizons and Spiral-Wake.*

Vaelith is the sea as conversation: the shore speaks, the deep replies, and ships
survive by learning when neither is interested in argument.

- **Cadence:** Seas, currents, storms, horizons, travel, and the bargains between
  water and land.
- **Sign:** Three wave-crests turning within a spiral shell.
- **Those who call Vaelith:** Sailors, coastfolk, tide-readers, navigators, and
  families who keep lamps for ships late returning.
- **Tenets:** Respect the deep. Share water. Spare hunger. A storm tests hull and
  heart but excuses cruelty in neither.
- **Observances:** The Casting of the Third Knot before departure; Spiral-Wake
  memorials after storms.
- **Entanglements:** Allied with Aelor over safe passage; locked in an old dispute
  with Thalassa over the price the sea may rightfully demand.

### Myrr, the Quiet Brook

*Also called the Deep-Rooted, Hand of Water, and Keeper of Open Doors.*

Myrr is the small mercy that outlives the great speech. Surface temples picture a
brook cupped in two hands. The hidden groves teach that every brook becomes root,
every root becomes forest, and no act of healing ends where it first touches the
world.

- **Cadence:** Peace, healing, sanctuary, groundwater, roots, interdependence, and
  unnoticed kindness.
- **Sign:** A cupped hand brimming with water, sometimes crossed beneath by three
  pale roots.
- **Those who call Myrr:** Healers, pacifists, gardeners, wayhouse keepers,
  mediators, and those who leave doors open during winter.
- **Tenets:** A life saved is a promise to the Loom. End a feud with bread and a
  story when either can still be heard. Never poison a well. Remember that roots
  drink what the surface forgets.
- **Observances:** Waterblessing at new wells; the Night of Open Doors at winter's
  first frost; the Root Communion known only to the Howling Moon's elder ranks.
- **Entanglements:** Cherished by Seraphine, hunted by Yurga's plague cults, and
  joined with Zorren and Orith in the Howling Moon's esoteric constellation.

### Zorren, Lord of the Wild Hunt

*Also called the Untamed, White Antler, and Last-Track.*

Zorren is hunger with restraint, freedom with consequence, the chase in which
predator and prey acknowledge one another as participants in the same old prayer.

- **Cadence:** Beasts, moonlit pursuit, freedom, primal triumph, and the hunter's
  bond.
- **Sign:** A white antler enclosed by a narrow crescent.
- **Those who call him:** Rangers, beast-tamers, wilderness guides, free companies,
  and all who understand that civilization is a fire, not a fence.
- **Tenets:** Hunt to live, never to glut. Give the fallen a song and the earth
  their bones. Break chains that pen the wind. The pack survives by remembering
  every member.
- **Observances:** Moonfeast at autumn's first full moon; Last-Track at year's end;
  the sharing of the Blood Bond after a necessary kill; and the monthly Wild
  Nights, when worshippers run beneath the full moon without rank or title.
- **Entanglements:** Public patron of the Howling Moon beside Selithiel; joined in
  its hidden rites by Myrr and Orith; defender of Viridara's groves; contemptuous
  of Ghorak's wasteful conquest.

### Lumerion, the Lantern-Bearer

*Also called Keeper of Crossroads, Coin-Wick, and the Kindly Chance.*

Lumerion is luck made ethical. His priests insist fortune is not a possession but
a lamp: carried briefly, borrowed often, and shameful when kept from those walking
in darkness.

- **Cadence:** Luck, roads, wayfinding, travel, and small chances arriving at the
  necessary hour.
- **Sign:** A hanging lantern whose wick curls like a smile.
- **Those who call him:** Wanderers, messengers, gamblers with generous hands,
  guides, refugees, and the simply lost.
- **Tenets:** Share the road and the map. Loose coins should find empty hands.
  Fortune hoarded curdles into accident.
- **Observances:** Lanternnight, when crossroads are lit and guarded until dawn;
  coin blessings before a journey.
- **Entanglements:** Companion to Pella, delighted victim of Brandoc's pranks, and
  persistent enemy of Sharguk's terror.

### Erix the Coinwright

*Also called Charter-Hand and the Founder's Seal.*

Erix governs the promise hidden inside exchange. A coin is only metal until two
strangers agree it means tomorrow will resemble today.

- **Cadence:** Trade, craft, contracts, cities, guilds, and durable works.
- **Sign:** A square coin stamped with two clasped hands.
- **Those who call him:** Merchants, builders, guilds, auditors, artisans, and
  rulers wise enough to fear an unjust market.
- **Tenets:** A just bargain enriches both. Build for centuries. Swindled wealth
  is haunted wealth. Every contract contains a person, however carefully the ink
  attempts to hide them.
- **Observances:** Market Consecration at each new charter; Founders' Audit after
  harvest.
- **Entanglements:** Respects Thalos, distrusts Vespera's guilds, and shares the
  Makers' Compact with Borhild and Pyrion.

### Vespera of the Many Masks

*Also called the Trickster, Low King's Jester, and the Second Name.*

Vespera teaches that power survives the sword more readily than laughter. Her
temples are theatres until the guards arrive, then kitchens, then empty rooms no
one remembers renting.

- **Cadence:** Trickery, performance, reinvention, illusion, subterfuge, and
  revolution through ridicule.
- **Sign:** A fan of three masks: laughing, weeping, and serene.
- **Those who call her:** Performers, spies, confidence artists, revolutionaries,
  satirists, shapeshifters, and those beginning a second life under a better name.
- **Tenets:** Every face is a mask; choose yours. Break tyranny with a joke people
  can remember. Meaning is the play, and truth is sometimes only a stage
  direction.
- **Observances:** The Masquerade of Low Kings at midwinter; the Rite of the Second
  Name; monthly Theatres of Truth that mock those most able to forbid them.
- **Entanglements:** Public patron of the Shattered Mirror beside Thalos; member of
  its hidden triad with Draven and Kaelthir; flirtatious rival to Nyxara; teacher
  and tormentor of Calystral. Street plays insist Lumerion has rescued Vespera
  from her own schemes more than once; her clergy repay the tale by making
  Sharguk's terror ridiculous wherever they can.

### Nethris, the Gravewarden

*Also called the Inevitable, Keeper of Keys, and Ledger's End.*

Nethris does not cause death. Nethris ensures death is not made meaningless by
those who remain. The Gravewarden is the final account, the proper name spoken at
the crossing, the door closed softly enough that grief may hear itself think.

- **Cadence:** Death, fate, final rites, natural endings, memory of the dead, and
  safe passage.
- **Sign:** A key tied to a raven feather by grey cord.
- **Those who call Nethris:** Undertakers, jurists, monks, execution witnesses,
  keepers of last wills, and families sitting the final vigil.
- **Tenets:** Every life is a ledger; settle it cleanly. The dead are owed silence
  and safe passage. Fate is a road, not a jailer. Do not turn grief into ownership.
- **Observances:** Crossing Vigils at dusk; Ledgerfast on the year's final day.
- **Entanglements:** Maintains cold courtesy with Myrr; condemns Yurga's abuse of
  endings; stands in both the public and esoteric constellations of the Pale
  Throne.

### Calystral, the Flameheart

*Also called the Passionate, Lamp of Muses, and Twinned Breath.*

Calystral is the fire that creates because destruction would be easier. Lovers,
artists, and rebels all claim the god favors them most. Calystral has never
settled the argument.

- **Cadence:** Love, art, passion, beauty, celebration, and creative fire.
- **Sign:** A heart-shaped flame inside a circlet of quills.
- **Those who call Calystral:** Artists, lovers, celebrants, patron-muses, and
  anyone who has chosen to make beauty while cruelty was available.
- **Tenets:** Create boldly. Love honestly. Forgive fiercely. Burn for what you
  would save, never merely for the pleasure of flame.
- **Observances:** The summer Night of a Thousand Lamps; Vows of Twinned Breath;
  roadside offerings of unfinished songs.
- **Entanglements:** One of the Ember Throne's three patrons with Pyrion and
  Borhild; patron to Lirael; implacable toward Velara's claim that murder can be
  art.

### Orith, the Stonefather

*Also called the Stone-Speaker, Echokeep, and Mountain Rune.*

Orith is patience with mass. Mountain folk say the god speaks constantly, but one
must listen for a century to hear a sentence.

- **Cadence:** Earth, mountains, endurance, shelter, patience, and the strength
  that does not need to announce itself.
- **Sign:** A mountain rune cut into unpolished granite.
- **Those who call Orith:** Miners, mountaineers, masons, refugees seeking shelter,
  and defenders who intend to remain after the songs are finished.
- **Tenets:** Move slowly and surely. Shelter the valley from the avalanche you
  begin. Let time become an ally. Strength that cannot bear weight is merely
  hardness.
- **Observances:** Stonebinding at the founding of halls; Echofast in solstice
  caverns.
- **Entanglements:** Close to Borhild; guardian of Viridara's roots; hidden patron
  of the Howling Moon and public patron of the Pale Throne.

## Dwarven Hearth and Forge

These names survive in surface halls, ancestral songs, and fragments carried into
Nagburim before its gates closed. Crystal Dwarf theologians disagree whether the
five are literal gods, ancestral harmonics, or old names for virtues that crystal
has made unnecessary. The disagreement itself has lasted longer than several
kingdoms.

### Borhild Emberforge

*The Maker, Anvil-Saint, and master of consecrated labor.*

- **Cadence:** Forgecraft, innovation, patient mastery, and work undertaken as a
  vow.
- **Sign:** An anvil marked by a single living ember.
- **Law:** Work honors those who taught the hand. Temper strength with purpose. A
  masterpiece is a promise in metal.
- **Entanglements:** Shares the Ember Throne with Pyrion and Calystral; builds the
  Makers' Compact beside Erix; trusts Orith's stone to remember every foundation.

### Skarn Graniteward

*The Tower-Shield and Keeper of Watches.*

- **Cadence:** Guardianship, vigilance, gates, walls, and the burden of remaining
  awake.
- **Sign:** A tower-shield bearing a square granite rune.
- **Law:** Be the wall, but remember whom the wall protects. An open gate may be
  negligence or welcome; wisdom lies in knowing the hour.

### Maela Rubyvein

*The Hearthmother and Lady of the Ruby Cup.*

- **Cadence:** Family, hearth, healing draughts, inheritance, and kin chosen as
  deliberately as kin born.
- **Sign:** A ruby cup filled with clear water.
- **Law:** Raise children brave and kind. A table binds a house as mortar binds
  stone. No lineage is strengthened by cruelty hidden as tradition.

### Duerak Deepdelve

*Lantern-and-Pick, Keeper of the Vein, and guardian of craft's quiet secrets.*

- **Cadence:** Subterranean lore, hidden veins, runes, discovery, and secrecy used
  to protect rather than impoverish.
- **Sign:** A hooded lantern crossed by a miner's pick.
- **Law:** Knowledge hoarded dulls; knowledge shared sharpens. Yet a map given to
  greed becomes a weapon against the mountain.

### Vangar Battlebraid

*The Oath of Steel and singer of worthy foes.*

- **Cadence:** Heroism, battle-fury, vengeance against treachery, and the names of
  the fallen.
- **Sign:** A braided knot laid over crossed blades.
- **Law:** Choose worthy enemies. Avenge betrayal, not embarrassment. Sing every
  name the victor would prefer erased.

## Elven Courts of Bough and Star

Elven prayers are rarely addressed upward. They are sung into pools, leaves,
moonlight, and the patient air between stars. Elves say the gods do not descend to
hear them; the worshipper learns to stand where the god has always been listening.

### Selithiel Moonbough

*The Dream-Walker, Mistress of Passage, and keeper of the crescent path.*

- **Cadence:** Moon, dreams, passage between worlds, mysticism, and the truths
  remembered only while waking.
- **Sign:** A silver crescent resting in a living bough.
- **Law:** Walk softly between worlds. Dreams remember what waking pride forgets.
- **Entanglements:** Public patron of the Howling Moon beside Zorren; shares the
  borders of dream with Fea without claiming the Titan's deeper dominion.

### Aerion Swiftwind

*Feather-Spiral and keeper of the messenger's oath.*

- **Cadence:** Air, migration, honest messages, weather roads, and necessary
  departures.
- **Sign:** A white feather curled into a spiral.
- **Law:** Bear tidings truly. A lie placed upon the wind returns as a storm.

### Viridara Thornsong

*Greenwarden, Oak-Heart, and the harmony that includes the thorn.*

- **Cadence:** Forests, growth, pruning, wild music, and the fierce mercy of living
  systems.
- **Sign:** An oak leaf wound with a flowering thorn.
- **Law:** Prune to heal. Root to endure. Seed to return. Growth without boundary
  becomes hunger wearing green.

### Lirael Dawnsong

*The Golden Lyre and Grace in Motion.*

- **Cadence:** Beauty, poetry, dance, craft of voice, and art offered as mercy.
- **Sign:** A small golden lyre strung with dawn-colored thread.
- **Law:** Talent is duty. Beauty is not innocence, but it may remind innocence
  how to return.
- **Entanglements:** Beloved of Calystral and occasionally employed by Vespera,
  though never with full disclosure.

### Kaelthir Starwarden

*The Thousand-Minded, Seven-Pointed, and Keeper of Old Names.*

- **Cadence:** Ancient knowledge, time, prophecy, divided thought, stars, and ideas
  that do not fit safely inside one mind.
- **Sign:** A seven-pointed star map with no fixed north.
- **Law:** Keep old names. Read the sky humbly. Warn without ruling. Knowledge that
  breaks its bearer must be carried by many hands or left sleeping.
- **Entanglements:** Member of the Shattered Mirror's hidden triad with Vespera and
  Draven; works beside Aethyra where memory becomes too large for one archive.

## Halfling Hearth-Tide

Halfling theology begins with the suspicion that any god demanding a cathedral
has misunderstood hospitality. Their shrines fit above doors, beside cookfires,
and in the polished pocket where a lucky coin has been rubbed thin.

### Pella of the Warm Hearth

*Kettle-and-Key and Keeper of the Returning Road.*

- **Cadence:** Home, hospitality, safe roads, shared food, and the lamp left in a
  window.
- **Sign:** A black kettle crossed by a brass key.
- **Law:** No guest goes hungry. Home is a promise, not a boundary.
- **Entanglements:** Companion to Lumerion and occasional mediator between Myrr's
  healers and families too proud to admit need.

### Brandoc Quickstep

*Double-Sixes, Door-Dancer, and patron of audacity with rules.*

- **Cadence:** Luck, wit, nimble theft, escape, and the prank that reveals a
  tyrant's weakness.
- **Sign:** A playing die showing six on both visible faces.
- **Law:** Steal from greed, never need. Never rob the table that fed you. A clever
  escape is wasted unless someone else finds the door left open.

### Willow Merryleaf

*The Garlanded Flute and keeper of harvest joy.*

- **Cadence:** Gardens, festivals, song, small abundance, and grief carried into
  dancing without being denied.
- **Sign:** A wooden flute wrapped in fresh leaves.
- **Law:** Joy is a harvest. Share it before winter.

## Orcish Ash-Legion

The Ash-Legion names its gods in challenge as often as in praise. A prayer may be
a plea, a boast, an accusation, or the promise that one day the worshipper will
become strong enough to stop praying.

### Ghorak the Ash-Eyed

*Breaker of Chains and lord of the strong ground.*

- **Cadence:** Conquest, survival, strength, command, and the violence by which
  one chain is broken and another too often forged.
- **Sign:** A charred eye above a broken spearshaft.
- **Law:** Take the strong ground. Honor the unbroken. Strength that destroys what
  it needs to survive is only fear with muscles.
- **Entanglements:** Hated by Zorren and Viridara for treating the living world as
  proof of conquest.

### Luthra Bloodmother

*The Red Hand and Keeper of the Last Fire.*

- **Cadence:** Caves, fertility, endurance of kin, hidden children, and the memory
  carried in blood.
- **Sign:** A red handprint surrounding one black coal.
- **Law:** Blood remembers. Hide the young. Keep one fire alive when every other
  light has been taken.

### Sharguk Nightfang

*First Terror and the black tooth before battle.*

- **Cadence:** Night raids, silence, intimidation, patient ambush, and fear shaped
  into strategy.
- **Sign:** A blackened fang without root or jaw.
- **Law:** Fear is a spear thrown before the body arrives. Leave no warning unless
  the warning itself serves the hunt.
- **Entanglements:** Opposed by Lumerion, mocked by Vespera, and quietly admired by
  Nyxaran sects that have forgotten the difference between secrecy and terror.

### Yurga of the Pale Hand

*The Rage-Drinker, Rot-Bringer, and the ending that refuses to end.*

- **Cadence:** Disease, rot, despair, wrathful death, vengeance beyond the grave,
  and suffering made contagious.
- **Sign:** A bone-white handprint scored through with red.
- **Law:** Let rot do the work. Make courage useless. Drink rage until the dead
  rise angry enough to remember who wronged them.
- **Entanglements:** Pursued by Nethris for violating proper endings; enemy of
  Myrr's sanctuaries; member of the Pale Throne's forbidden inner trinity.

## Seafolk and Sky

The sea has more prayers than shores. Vaelith belongs to every coast, but these two
names are strongest among those who make their homes upon water rather than beside
it.

### Thalassa Stormqueen

*The Crowned Wave and keeper of the Tenth Sail.*

- **Cadence:** Tempests, shipwrecks, deep truths, tribute, and the price the sea
  demands without explaining.
- **Sign:** A breaking wave wearing a crown of foam.
- **Law:** The sea is a debt; pay it when asked. Her priesthood's demand that great
  fleets surrender a tenth sail remains one of Lumia's most disputed holy customs.
- **Entanglements:** Contends with Vaelith over whether the sea may demand payment
  from those it has already frightened, and with Aelor over how much mercy a storm
  owes a sailor.

### Aelor Keelwarden

*Compass Rose, Star-Swear, and guardian of crews homeward bound.*

- **Cadence:** Sailors, fair winds, honest charts, command at sea, and safe return.
- **Sign:** A compass rose enclosed by a ship's wheel.
- **Law:** Bring crews home. Swear by stars that can witness the oath. A false chart
  is murder committed before the voyage begins.
- **Entanglements:** Allied with Vaelith where currents aid passage; forever
  negotiating with Thalassa, who considers safety a luxury the sea never promised.

## The Under-Shadow Courts

These gods are worshipped beneath cities, behind thrones, and in those private
rooms where respectable people arrange for disrespectable necessities. Their
temples deny being temples. Their priests deny being priests. The denials are part
of the liturgy.

### Velara, the Obsidian Rose

*Lady of the Silent Court and keeper of the blood contract.*

- **Cadence:** Political assassination, veiled ambition, silent courts, elegance,
  and power that prefers not to be seen holding the knife.
- **Sign:** A black rose whose thorns point inward.
- **Law:** Power is a contract written in blood. Elegance is the blade no witness
  remembers seeing.
- **Entanglements:** Condemned by Calystral, who rejects murder's claim to beauty;
  courted by Nyxaran extremists and distrusted even by them.

### Zhaerin Nightglass

*Mirror of Unmemory and keeper of forbidden pages.*

- **Cadence:** Shadow magic, stolen memory, forbidden texts, erased histories, and
  knowledge that protects itself by consuming the reader.
- **Sign:** A dark mirror that reflects writing but not faces.
- **Law:** Knowledge kept is power doubled. Erase what can erase you—but remember
  that every erasure leaves a shape.
- **Entanglements:** Adversary to Aethyra in the Memory Wars; cautious ally of
  Nyxara; rival to Kaelthir over whether dangerous knowledge should be divided or
  destroyed.

### Draven Coil

*The Forbidden, Chalice-Viper, and lord of patient ruin.*

- **Cadence:** Poisons, subversion, forbidden knowledge, slow corruption, secrets
  that seek escape, and victory achieved before an enemy knows there was a war.
- **Sign:** A coiled serpent drinking from an overturned chalice.
- **Law:** If a fortress cannot be toppled, study its roots. Every poison is a
  lesson in proportion. Knowledge forced into silence learns to bite.
- **Entanglements:** Opposes Seraphine through corrupted harvests; member of the
  Shattered Mirror's hidden triad with Vespera and Kaelthir.

## The Elemental Primarchs

The Primarchs are gods at the edge of definition. Their influence is older than
most language, yet they now answer to names, receive worship, and resonate through
the Loom. Some theologians call them the first Woven Powers. Others say they are
the world's oldest forces taught to wear faces.

Neither account explains why their shrines sometimes remember weather that has not
happened yet.

### Pyrion, the First Flame

*The Primal, Forge-Heart, and White Spiral.*

- **Cadence:** Primal fire, creation through destruction, forge heat, renewal, and
  passion purified of pretense.
- **Sign:** A spiral of white flame consuming its own tail.
- **Those who call Pyrion:** Elemental fire-workers, smiths of enchanted metal,
  artists who distrust gentle inspiration, and the Ember Throne.
- **Law:** Burn away what must end, not what merely resists you. Passion without
  purpose is smoke. The forge teaches patience through heat.
- **Entanglements:** One of the Ember Throne's three patrons; rival to Glacius;
  allied with Borhild and Erix in the Makers' Compact.

Pyrion's herald is commonly called the **Ember Titan**, a giant of living flame
and molten metal. Here *titan* is a title of magnitude, not membership among the
Twenty Deep Titans.

### Glacius, the Eternal Winter

*Perfect Snow and the Still End.*

- **Cadence:** Primal ice, preservation, patience, suspended time, and endings
  made beautiful by stillness.
- **Sign:** A perfect snowflake that does not melt in an open hand.
- **Those who call Glacius:** Ice-workers, preservers of ancient knowledge,
  undertakers, winter travelers, and the Pale Throne.
- **Law:** All things end. Preserve what matters, not merely what can be kept.
  Patience outlasts passion but must not mistake delay for wisdom.
- **Entanglements:** Eternal counterpoint to Pyrion; works with Nethris on endings;
  public patron of the Pale Throne beside Nethris and Orith; respects Kaelthir's
  patience with the long memory of stars.

### Verdania, the Growing Green

*The Spiral Tree and Untamed Life.*

- **Cadence:** Primal growth, regeneration, wild abundance, hunger, and life that
  refuses the shape prepared for it.
- **Sign:** A tree whose roots and branches form one endless spiral.
- **Those who call Verdania:** Druids, rangers, seed-keepers, healers of blighted
  land, and those who have seen vines split stone.
- **Law:** Life finds a way, but not every way is mercy. Growth requires nurture
  and pruning in equal honesty.
- **Entanglements:** Patron of Viridara's forests, companion to Zorren in matters
  of the hunt, opposed to Umbros where rest becomes stagnation.

### Umbros, the Deep Dark

*The Void-Keeper, Stillness, and the space between stars.*

- **Cadence:** Primal darkness, violent death, sudden endings, rest, absence,
  shadow consumption, and knowledge hidden beyond light.
- **Sign:** A sphere of darkness that dims the surface on which it is drawn.
- **Those who call Umbros:** Deep-cave dwellers, scholars of absence, keepers of
  dangerous rest, and the Pale Throne's innermost ranks.
- **Law:** In darkness all things lose the titles by which pride separated them.
  Some knowledge must sleep. Rest is sacred until it becomes surrender.
- **Entanglements:** Ancient ally of Nyxara; counterforce to every uncompromising
  light; member of the Pale Throne's hidden death trinity with Nethris and Yurga;
  watched cautiously by Duerak's deep-delving faithful.

## The Knightly Constellations

Knight records appear to contradict one another about divine patronage. They do
not. They record different depths of initiation.

Every recognized Order has a **public constellation**, named in vows, recruitment,
and ordinary worship. Several also possess an **esoteric constellation**, revealed
only when a knight's transformation requires powers the public doctrine cannot
safely explain. A deity may stand in more than one constellation without division;
the sky does not lose a star because two travelers use it to navigate.

| Knight Order | Public constellation | Esoteric constellation | The distinction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Crimson Loom | Kordran and Aethyra | Kordran and Aethyra | Its secret is not different patrons but the intimate bond between war, oath, and the two gods themselves. |
| Sundered Dawn | Seraphine and Nyxara | Seraphine and Nyxara | Its deeper mystery is Luxumbra and the sisters' approaching reunion. |
| Ember Throne | Pyrion, Calystral, and Borhild | Pyrion, Calystral, and Borhild | Fire, passion, and craft are disclosed early because the initiation cannot hide what it burns. |
| Howling Moon | Zorren and Selithiel | Zorren, Myrr, and Orith | Selithiel guides the crossing through moon and dream; the inner rites transform the knight through beast, root, and stone. |
| Shattered Mirror | Vespera and Thalos | Vespera, Draven, and Kaelthir | Thalos gives the public discipline of truth and law; the hidden triad fractures identity through deception, forbidden knowledge, and divided thought. |
| Pale Throne | Nethris, Glacius, and Orith | Nethris, Umbros, and Yurga | The public path teaches natural ending, stillness, and endurance; the inner path confronts gentle death, sudden death, and wrathful death. |
| Void Crown | The Unnamed | Unknown, absent, or impossible | Its patron is called the Un-God, Absent Throne, and Paradox Divine. The distinction between public and secret may have vanished with the Order itself. |

This layered patronage also explains the gods' differing epithets in knightly
records. Myrr the Quiet Brook becomes Myrr the Deep-Rooted in the Howling rites.
Draven Coil becomes Draven the Forbidden before the Shattered Mirror. Yurga of the
Pale Hand becomes the Rage-Drinker when the Pale Throne crosses its final death.
A title is not an alias of convenience. It is the face a divine cadence turns
toward a particular oath.

## Divine Resonances

When gods share worship, purpose, or opposition, their cadences form stable
resonances in the Loom:

- **The Light and Shadow Dyad:** Seraphine and Nyxara, divided from Luxumbra and
  moving slowly toward reunion.
- **The Forge Trinity:** Borhild, Pyrion, and Erix—skill, force, and exchange joined
  in creation.
- **The Death Quartet:** Nethris, Glacius, Umbros, and Yurga—proper ending,
  preserved ending, sudden ending, and ending made wrath.
- **The Nature Circle:** Viridara, Verdania, Orith, Myrr, and Zorren—leaf, growth,
  stone, root-water, and beast maintaining an order none controls alone.
- **The Mercy Circle:** Myrr, Seraphine, and Pella, whose temples maintain a quiet
  agreement that no war may close every door.
- **The Shadow Pact:** Nyxara, Umbros, and Vespera, who trade in concealment for
  reasons each insists the others misunderstand.

Resonance is neither friendship nor obedience. Two bells may harmonize while
hanging in enemy towers.

## Worship, Clergy, and Sacred Practice

Clergy describe an ordinary petition as **knotting a thread**. The petitioner
names a need, a virtue by which the need should be answered, and a memory offered
in witness. A need without virtue is appetite. A virtue without memory is a word
the Loom has no reason to trust.

Each tradition also keeps a sacred hand-sign by which its clergy may identify one
another in silence. Some are public blessings. Others are taught only beneath a
closed door, and several pairs of hostile sects claim the same gesture with the
fingers reversed.

### The Five Prayers

Across otherwise hostile traditions, clergy recognize five ordinary moments of
address:

1. **Dawn:** gratitude for what returned.
2. **Noon:** dedication of the day's strength.
3. **Dusk:** accounting for what was done.
4. **Midnight:** vigilance for what moves unseen.
5. **Crisis:** the prayer without preparation, spoken when the world has narrowed
   to one need.

No doctrine requires all five. Almost every life eventually speaks the last.

### Orders of Service

Most organized temples recognize initiates, acolytes, priests, high priests, and
the rare **Chosen** who has survived direct divine attention. Titles vary by
people. Duties do not: keep the rites, preserve the stories, aid the faithful,
challenge corruption within the temple, and remember that a god's appetite is not
automatically a moral law.

Vestments differ wildly, but shared thread and knot symbols allow rival clergy to
recognize one another under truce. This custom began after the Memory Wars, when
three priests bled to death arguing over which of them had authority to heal the
fourth.

Temples follow the character of the name they hold. Thalos and Kordran favor
cathedrals whose stones make authority visible. Nyxara and Vespera prefer shrines
that can disappear before accusation arrives. Viridara and Zorren accept a grove
as both roof and scripture. None of these forms proves greater devotion. Gods,
like rain, have never been impressed by ceilings.

### Schools Within Temples

No living faith speaks with one mouth. The better-known schools include:

- **Kordran's Banner-Menders**, who refuse siege warfare and hold that duty begins
  with defending those a campaign would make invisible.
- **Nyxara's Lamplighters**, who shelter the secrets of the persecuted and accuse
  other Nyxaran sects of confusing concealment with ownership.
- **Seraphine's Dusk Walkers**, who teach that redemption is real only after it
  has crossed difficulty in full knowledge of the cost.
- **Thalos's Grey Judges**, who seek rehabilitation before punishment and keep a
  second ledger for harms prevented by mercy.
- **Myrr's Silent Hands**, who take vows of silence and make healing itself their
  argument.

Temple councils call these differences schisms when angry, schools when calm,
and ancient tradition when soliciting donations from their adherents.

### Knots and Pilgrimage

Sacred places are called **Knots**, points where repeated memory has tightened the
Loom:

- crossroads lit for Lumerion;
- cavern springs kept for Myrr;
- mountain echoes answered in Orith's name;
- moonlit clearings run beneath Zorren and Selithiel;
- seaside cliffs where Vaelith's third knot is cast into the wind;
- market stones audited for Erix and Thalos;
- thresholds left unmarked for Nyxara;
- dawn fields planted for Seraphine.

At such places the simplest act carries doctrine: a lantern lit for Lumerion, a
thread bound for Aethyra, a rune inscribed for Thalos, water poured for Myrr, a
secret surrendered to Nyxara, or a seed pressed into earth for Seraphine. Many
clergy undertake a lifetime pilgrimage to three Knots, choosing one familiar, one
foreign, and one whose god they fear.

Once each century, clergy from many traditions gather at the First Temple for the
Great Convergence. No reliable source agrees where the First Temple stands. This
has not prevented seven cities from hosting the gathering simultaneously and
producing miracles in all seven places.

### Omens

During great miracles, Loom-threads may hum audibly and the sky may take on the
patron's character: gold at Seraphine's intervention, starless black around
Nyxara, impossible written patterns near Aethyra. Red tides, singing stones, and
dream-storms belong to several gods at once and have started more theological
disputes than they have settled. Lesser signs are more intimate: a lantern that
refuses to die, a cup that will not spill, a flower opening in winter, a shadow
leaning against the light.

Divine displeasure is quieter. Holy signs tarnish. Familiar prayers feel hollow.
The road remains, but something at its far end has turned away.

## Relics and Stories Made Solid

The following are the best-attested great relics. Every temple claims more. Most
such claims begin shortly before a request for funding.

- **Aethyra's Spindle** is said to reweave a broken oath or restore one memory
  truly lost.
- **Kordran's Sunhammer** refuses the hand that seeks battle only to be praised for
  winning it.
- **Seraphine's Seven Seeds** can return life to land that has forgotten how to
  grow.
- **Nyxara's Veil** hides its wearer from every search except the searcher they
  secretly wish would find them.
- **Thalos's Ledger** records every lie spoken in its presence, including those
  written by its owner.
- **Nethris's Key** opens a way between life and death. The stories disagree about
  which direction is more difficult.

Smaller relics pass more often through mortal hands: single Threads of the Loom,
Waystone Lanterns, Mercy Cups, Truth Chalk, Dawn Crystals, and Shadow Cloaks. Their
power is less important than their provenance. A cup carried through three
plagues may heal because Myrr blessed it—or because generations of frightened
hands taught it what a cup should do.

## Schisms, Alliances, and Divine Weather

### The Great Schisms

- **The Dawn Sundering:** Luxumbra divided into Seraphine and Nyxara, creating a
  wound between revelation and concealment that still opens every morning and
  closes every night.
- **The Forge War:** Borhild and Pyrion contested whether creation belongs to the
  hand or the flame. Erix ended the first conflict by asking who owned the finished
  blade. The second conflict began immediately.
- **The Silent Accord:** Nethris and Myrr agreed that healing and death must coexist
  without either claiming every soul. Their most devout followers still disagree
  about the agreement's exact wording.

### Active Conflicts

- **The Harvest Dispute:** Seraphine's clergy oppose Draven's cults wherever slow
  poison enters cropland.
- **The Sea Divided:** Vaelith, Thalassa, and Aelor contend over dominion, tribute,
  and the meaning of safe passage.
- **The Memory Wars:** Aethyra preserves what Zhaerin would steal or erase.
- **The Wild Boundaries:** Zorren and Viridara resist Ghorak's claim that strength
  confers ownership over the living world.

### Prophecies Held in Common

- **The Thread's End:** The Loom will unravel unless every divine cadence finds a
  moment of unity.
- **The Second Dawn:** Seraphine and Nyxara will reunite, though no prophecy agrees
  whether Luxumbra's return will be healing or annihilation.
- **The Final Hunt:** Zorren's last chase will decide whether wildness remains a
  living freedom or becomes only memory.
- **The Great Audit:** Thalos will judge the gods. One forbidden version claims the
  gods created Thalos precisely because they feared no one else could.

## Heralds and Servitors

Gods rarely manifest without consequence. More often they send a herald: a being
shaped by the deity's cadence but capable of walking the mortal world without
tearing it like wet paper.

The best-attested heralds are:

- **Aethyra's Memory Sphinx:** A crystalline keeper that answers questions with
  memories belonging to someone else.
- **Kordran's Dawn Champion:** An armored figure of living sunlight carrying a
  hammer that grows heavier in an unjust cause.
- **Seraphine's Golden Phoenix:** A bird of renewal whose feathers smell of wheat
  after rain.
- **Nyxara's Shadow Oracle:** A veiled figure that knows every secret except why it
  was commanded to speak.
- **Thalos's Unblinking Judge:** A construct of law that cannot be deceived and has
  therefore learned to fear incomplete evidence.
- **Nethris's Pale Ferryman:** A silent guide who accepts no coin, only the final
  truth a passenger has avoided.
- **Myrr's Mercy Dove:** A luminous bird seen near the dying and beside doors that
  open unexpectedly in winter.
- **Zorren's Alpha Wolf:** A silver-eyed beast that hunts those who kill without
  need.
- **Lumerion's Waylight:** A cheerful lantern-spirit that helps the lost and
  occasionally misleads those who deserved to become lost for an hour.

The Primarchs send stranger servants: Pyrion's Ember Titan, Glacius's Frost Wyrm,
Verdania's walking World Tree, and Umbros's Void Walker. Lesser orders include
Aethyra's Thread Weavers, Kordran's Banner Bearers, Seraphine's Dawn Maidens,
Nyxara's Veil Keepers, Thalos's Law Scribes, Nethris's Soul Guides, Myrr's Brook
Spirits, and Zorren's Hunt Hounds.

## The Question Behind Every Altar

The Shattered Truth proposes that every deity may be mortal consciousness trying
to reconstruct the divine unity lost when the Aeon broke. If this is so, prayer is
not a lesser being begging a greater one for aid. It is one fragment calling
through darkness to another, each hoping the answering voice remembers more of
the song.

Other theologians reject this. They point to manifestations, relics, divine
anger, and miracles no mortal expected or desired. The gods are real, they say,
because they surprise us.

Aethyran scholars answer that dreams are real and surprise their dreamers.

The argument remains.

The prayers continue.

### Reconciliation decisions

This canon document adopts the following reconciliations:

- Gods are Loom-anchored personal powers; Titans are dominion-anchored powers of
  a separate order. The Elemental Primarchs remain a deliberately disputed edge
  case.
- The patron lists in `KNIGHTS.md` are the Orders' public constellations.
- The differing patron lists in `divine_knight_concordance.md` are esoteric
  constellations revealed through advanced initiation.
- Myrr's Quiet Brook and Deep-Rooted traditions are two faces of one deity.
- Draven's poison and forbidden-knowledge traditions are one doctrine of patient
  corruption.
- Yurga's plague and wrathful-death traditions are one doctrine of suffering that
  refuses a clean ending.
- Pyrion's Ember Titan is a herald whose title describes magnitude; it is not one
  of the Twenty Titans.
- Nineteen names already occur in canon but receive substantial new religious
  detail here: Aethyra, Kordran, Seraphine, Nyxara, Thalos, Myrr, Zorren,
  Vespera, Nethris, Calystral, Orith, Borhild, Selithiel, Kaelthir, Draven,
  Yurga, Pyrion, Glacius, and Umbros.
- Twenty-one regional powers are established by this concordance: Vaelith,
  Lumerion, Erix, Skarn, Maela, Duerak, Vangar, Aerion, Viridara, Lirael, Pella,
  Brandoc, Willow, Ghorak, Luthra, Sharguk, Thalassa, Aelor, Velara, Zhaerin, and
  Verdania.

### Canon and runtime boundary

This document establishes divine identity, worship, relationships, rites,
religious institutions, relic traditions, and Knight patronage. It assigns no
numeric bonuses, spell access, combat effects, alignment restrictions, engine
identifiers, or implementation constants. Runtime mechanics remain a separate
system unless a future source explicitly binds them to this lore.
