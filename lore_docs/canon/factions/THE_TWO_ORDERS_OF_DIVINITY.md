# The Two Orders of Divinity

*A concordance of gods, Titans, and the dangerous little word **divine**, copied
from the Grey Archive of Aethyra with corrections attributed to three hands and
agreed upon by none of them.*

> *A god is a song the world has learned to sing.*
>
> *A Titan is the deep place where the echo goes.*
>
> *Both may answer prayer. This does not make them the same.*

## The Error in a Single Word

Mortals are fond of naming two things alike when both are large enough to frighten
them.

They call the light of Seraphine divine. They call the cold attention of Iluai
divine. They speak of a knight receiving divine fire, a Genasi receiving divine
favor, and a sailor cursing the divine powers that sent a storm. The word is useful
in the manner of a bucket: it carries much, provided no one asks what has been
mixed inside it.

The oldest concordances therefore use **divine** as a description of power, not
of nature. Divine power is power shaped by a will greater than the mortal who
channels it. The will may belong to a god. It may belong to a Titan. The miracle
may look the same in the hand, just as rain gathered from a roof resembles rain
drawn from a river.

The sources are not the same.

Neither are the minds behind them.

## The First Order: The Woven Gods

The beings commonly called gods or deities are known in the oldest clerical
records as the **Woven Powers**. Whatever their first origin—and temples have
burned one another over less—their present existence is bound to the Loom of
Aether.

Each god is a cadence within that greater weave: a pattern of name, memory,
purpose, and relationship held together until it can speak with a single voice.
Aethyra is not merely magic, nor Kordran merely war. Aethyra is magic remembered
and promised. Kordran is force given duty. Nethris is not death itself, but the
meaning mortals give to endings: the last rite, the settled account, the hand that
closes the final door gently and does not open it again without cause.

This is why gods require anchors.

An oath repeated across generations gives a deity purchase in the world. A holy
name spoken at dawn gives it shape. A Knight Order, carrying the same vows in
thousands of living hearts, becomes a rope cast across the gulf between idea and
flesh. Through such anchors gods manifest, grant miracles, suffer wounds, divide,
reunite, and sometimes drift toward abstraction when their last names are
forgotten.

The Sundered Dawn knows this more intimately than most. Seraphine and Nyxara were
once named as one. Now they are two. One day they may be one again. A Titan may
change its temper, its servants, even the face it shows the world; no accepted
record describes a Titan dividing because a story required two endings.

Gods are personal not because they are kind, but because personhood is the vessel
that holds them.

## The Second Order: The Deep Titans

The Titans are called the **Deep Powers** in records old enough that the ink has
turned the color of dried rain.

Each Titan is anchored first not to a mortal institution but to a dominion of its
own—a closed reach of reality that arcanists call a pocket plane and priests call
a throne beyond the world. A Titan may cross from that dominion into Lumia, or
open a narrow way through which power can pass. It may also close that way. This
is why a Titan's displeasure can end a miracle as cleanly as a door shutting in a
silent house.

The Titans do not require worship in order to exist. The surviving evidence
suggests they existed before their first temples and may have existed before the
Loom took its present form. Worship does, however, grant them **reach**. Prayer is
a road worn through the world. Reverence widens it. Repetition lays stone upon
stone until a distant power can walk where once it could only watch.

This distinction has misled more than one theologian:

- A god without mortal anchors may lose coherence and drift into abstraction.
- A Titan without worship remains itself, but its grip upon Lumia weakens.
- A god is made present through relationship.
- A Titan is made near through attention.

Neither condition makes one order stronger than the other. A mountain is not
greater than a promise because it is older. A promise is not greater than a
mountain because it can be broken.

The archives agree that there are **Twenty Titans**, though they do not agree on
all twenty names. The number remains strangely constant while the litanies vary,
as though mortal memory can misplace the notes but not the shape of the chord.

## A Scholar's Table of Necessary Differences

| Question | The Woven Gods | The Deep Titans |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What anchors them? | Names, vows, worship, memory, and mortal institutions woven through the Loom | Their own pocket dominions and the fundamental conditions those dominions contain |
| What does worship provide? | Coherence, identity, and a living tether to the physical world | Reach, influence, and a wider road into Lumia |
| How do they grant power? | They shape a Loom-thread through relationship, oath, and ritual | They open their dominion and permit a mortal to draw through it |
| How do they treat mortals? | Personally, though not always mercifully; mortals help define who they are | Remotely and transactionally; mortals are servants, instruments, curiosities, or temporary concerns |
| Can their portfolios overlap? | Frequently; a god embodies the meaning and practice surrounding a thing | Yes; a Titan holds the deep condition beneath the thing |
| What happens when forgotten? | The god risks becoming abstract, divided, diminished, or unmoored | The Titan grows distant but does not cease to be itself |
| How many are there? | Many, with names and identities capable of division, union, and change | Twenty, according to every surviving complete tradition |

This table is a map, not the country. It is useful precisely until it is not.

## The Two Roads of Divine Magic

Arcane scholars once argued that divine magic could have only one source because
its gestures, invocations, and visible effects followed a common grammar. Their
error was understandable. Two letters written with the same alphabet need not
have been sent by the same hand.

The Luminari taught mortals a method of calling power into form. Gods and Titans
both learned—or chose—to answer through that method, because a road already built
is easier to travel than wilderness.

### The Woven Road

A cleric of a god knots devotion, memory, and oath into the Loom. The patron deity
shapes that thread according to its nature and sends the miracle back along the
same bond. The god is not merely a reservoir. It is interpreter, author, and
witness.

This is the power carried by the Knight Orders. Their patrons can resonate through
them because the knights are living anchors, each oath another tightened cord.
When an order breaks faith, the wound travels in both directions.

### The Deep Road

A servant of a Titan opens a connection to the Titan's pocket dominion. The Titan
permits substance, force, or miracle to cross and shapes it before it reaches the
mortal world. Healing comes readily when a vast intelligence prepares the pattern
before the supplicant receives it. Raw elemental power may come less readily, for
even a Titan must draw beyond its own dominion when the petition exceeds its
nature.

This is the older meaning behind the claim that a Titan may grant or withhold
divine power at will. The claim is true. It is merely not the whole truth about
every divine caster.

### The Price of Too Much Nearness

Mortals who channel a Titan too deeply may become **Titan Blessed**. The phrase is
not a general title for saints. It names a specific transformation: the channel
widens until the mortal becomes part doorway, part echo, and at last an avatar
through which the Deep Power can act.

Knights who begin to resemble their patron gods undergo a related but distinct
danger. They are not being hollowed into doors. They are becoming additional
verses of the same divine song.

The difference comforts theologians more than it comforts those undergoing either
transformation.

## Why Death Has Two Masters

The dispute between the cults of Iluai and Nethris has produced twelve schisms,
three small wars, and one famously courteous poisoning.

It has not produced a contradiction.

**Iluai** is the Titan of Death: the Deep Power whose dominion touches death as a
condition, a force, and a gate through which power may be drawn. New Anteria's
Death Compact was made with Iluai because the Compact required authority over the
state of being dead. It was not a funeral prayer. It was an alteration of what
death permitted.

**Nethris** is the Gravewarden: the god of proper endings, final rites, fate, and
safe passage. Nethris governs what death means to mortals and what the living owe
the dead. A Nethrite priest may condemn the cruelty of an Iluain bargain while
acknowledging that the Titan possesses the power to make it.

Iluai is the dark water.

Nethris is the ferryman, the fare, and the promise that the boat will reach the
other shore.

This same pattern explains other apparent duplications. A Titan may hold the deep
country of dream while a god tends prophecy, nightmare, moonlit passage, or the
stories mortals bring back upon waking. A Titan may hold the sea's raw dominion
while Vaelith, Thalassa, and Aelor contend over storms, tribute, sailors, and safe
return.

Portfolios are not parcels of land. The divine do not respect fences drawn by
clerks.

## Fea, the False Fea, and the Shape of Belief

Fea is remembered as the Dream Titan. The False Fea calls this a lie of flesh. It
argues that a true power of dream should itself be made from dreams, and thousands
have slept their faith into that argument.

Here the distinction between the two orders becomes dangerous.

Belief can weave a personality into the Loom. Enough repeated stories can give a
mask memory, hunger, and eventually a voice. The so-called False Titans are such
things: **Woven echoes shaped in imitation of Deep Powers**. They are godlike in
their manner of becoming and Titan-like in the identities they attempt to steal.

They are not harmless copies. Every prayer offered to a False Titan lays a road in
the wrong direction. The true Titan remains in its dominion, but its grip upon
Lumia weakens as mortal attention is diverted to the echo. Thus the False
Pantheon can replace the Twenty in the world without ever becoming what the
Twenty are.

The False Fea is a nightmare with a name, and names are doors.

## The Twenty-First Titan

Gods may divide. Gods may merge. New gods may gather around new names, though the
process is rare and seldom gentle.

Since the Twenty became known as Twenty, no accepted history records a Titan
multiplying.

That is why the Twenty-First Titan is not merely another divine claimant. It is an
offense against the category itself: a Woven possibility attempting to become a
Deep necessity, a god-shaped absence seeking a dominion that has never existed.

The title **Unborn God** and the title **Twenty-First Titan** are therefore both
accurate. The thing is godlike in its formation and Titan-like in its ambition.
It feeds upon prayers to the Twenty because those prayers trace the boundary it
means to cross. It feeds upon doubt because doubt loosens every name and opens a
space between one certainty and the next.

Some say it is the Prisoner wearing a future mask. Some say it is the lost unity
of the Twenty seeking a single face. Others claim it is nothing more than the
shadow cast by a question mortals should never have asked.

The records are unclear.

The fear is not.

## Jal'Kara and the Courtesy of Sailors

The Forgotten Tide offers no worship to the Titans, yet grants professional
courtesy to **Jal'Kara**. Tide captains refuse to explain the distinction. Their
usual answer is that one worships what one hopes will love them, while one shows
courtesy to anything capable of swallowing the ship.

The oldest surviving Titan litany names **Orolro, the Sea-Wayfarer**, where Tide
records name Jal'Kara. Three interpretations persist:

1. Jal'Kara is the Tide's private name for Orolro, spoken only beyond sight of
   land.
2. Jal'Kara is a pact-name: not the Titan's identity, but the name under which a
   particular bargain was made.
3. Jal'Kara is another power entirely, and the Tide knows something the temples do
   not.

No interpretation has been proven. Tide ships continue to vanish less often than
the ships of those who laugh at the courtesy.

Among sailors, this is considered evidence enough.

## On Lesser Titans, Primarchs, and Careless Capital Letters

Not every being called a titan belongs to the Twenty.

In common speech the word also means a creature of overwhelming size or elemental
force. Pyrion's **Ember Titan**, for example, is named among the elemental heralds.
The title describes a giant of living flame and molten metal. It does not place
that herald among the Deep Powers.

The Elemental Primarchs complicate every clean taxonomy. Some scholars place them
among the oldest Woven Gods. Others call them conditions of the world that have
learned divine names. A third school insists they are bridges between the two
orders: too personal to be Titans, too ancient to depend entirely upon mortal
memory.

The Grey Concordance offers no ruling. A system that cannot admit an uncertain
edge is not scholarship. It is architecture pretending the weather does not
exist.

## The Age of Renewed Attention

The oldest dependable histories record no open Titan intervention before the
making of the Prison. Whether the Titans truly ignored mortal life or whether
earlier witnesses lacked the language to name them is unknown.

After the Prison was raised, the Deep Powers began to watch.

This is sensible even if it is not comforting. The Prison is made from Arcanite,
the crystallized residue of a shattered divine power. Its Locks bind Memory,
Identity, Time, Space, and Will. Such a structure presses against the foundations
of reality—the very depths the Titans are thought to inhabit.

Now Arcanite is being mined. The Locks weaken. The Shadowmoon approaches more
frequently. Roads of prayer are being cut toward false versions of the Twenty.

The Titans are taking interest in mortal affairs because mortal affairs have
begun to reach them.

## Three Theories of Origin

The concordance distinguishes gods from Titans by observable behavior. It does not
pretend to know which came first, or why either exists.

### The Two Heavens Doctrine

Gods and Titans are separate orders that arose independently: one bound to the
Loom, one anchored beyond it. Their similarities are the result of mortals using
the same prayers and the same word for both.

### The Doctrine of Masks

Gods are mortal-facing identities woven around powers too deep to approach
directly. Under this doctrine, several gods may interpret one Titan's dominion,
and one god may draw upon several Deep Powers. The gods reject this teaching when
asked, which proves either its falsehood or its importance.

### The Shattered Reconstruction

Titans are fundamental aspects of reality that preceded the Aeon's breaking.
Gods are the many fragments of mortal consciousness attempting, through worship,
to remember the divine unity that was lost. The Titans endure because the world's
conditions endure. The gods endure because the separated continue to call for one
another in the dark.

All three theories explain some evidence.

None explains why the Titans are twenty.

## The Archivist's Concordance

The following points are the narrow ground upon which the temples, Knight Orders,
and less quarrelsome academies can stand together:

- **Divine** is an umbrella term for power shaped by a greater will.
- **Gods** are personal powers bound to the Loom through names, relationships,
  memory, worship, and mortal anchors.
- **Titans** are remote powers anchored in pocket dominions; worship strengthens
  their reach into Lumia rather than creating their identities.
- Both orders can receive worship, grant miracles, withhold power, manifest, and
  share overlapping concerns.
- Titan-granted and god-granted miracles use a common magical grammar but do not
  travel from the same source.
- The **Titan Blessed** are direct conduits of Titans, not a general class of
  deity-touched mortals.
- The **False Titans** are Woven imitations that divert worship from the Deep
  Powers they mimic.
- The **Twenty-First Titan** is dangerous because it attempts to cross from a
  godlike possibility into a new fundamental dominion.
- Iluai's authority over death does not displace Nethris's authority over endings,
  rites, and passage.
- The exact origins of both orders—and their ultimate relationship—remain matters
  of theology rather than settled history.

These distinctions do not answer every question. They answer the questions that
otherwise tear holes in the record.

That is enough for a concordance.

It is not enough for the truth.

### Adopted resolutions

- **Divine** is the approved umbrella term for power shaped by a greater will;
  it does not make gods and Titans one kind of being.
- The Woven Gods are anchored through the Loom and mortal relationship. The Deep
  Titans are anchored in their own dominions, while worship extends their reach
  into Lumia.
- God-granted and Titan-granted miracles share a magical grammar learned through
  the Luminari method, but they do not share an ultimate source.
- The Twenty Titans, Titan Blessed, False Titans, and Twenty-First Titan retain
  the meanings defined in this concordance.
- Iluai and Nethris may both hold authority around death because a deep condition
  and its mortal meaning are not the same office.
- The relationship between Jal'Kara and Orolro, the status of the Elemental
  Primarchs, and the ultimate origins of either divine order remain deliberately
  unresolved.

### Canon and runtime boundary

This document establishes metaphysical identity, divine magic's two sources,
religious terminology, and the interpretation of existing Titan traditions. It
does not assign spell lists, numeric effects, alignment restrictions, engine
identifiers, or implementation constants.
