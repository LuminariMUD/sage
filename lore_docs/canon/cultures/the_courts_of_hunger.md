_From the redacted testimony of Sera Venn, licensed physician to the night

> Hunger does not make a monster.
>
> Hunger makes a question.

> The answer does the rest.

## Life Beyond Its Measure

Vampirism is often called undeath because the living prefer their categories to
remain clean.

It is not clean. It is not properly death.

A vampire is life held past its lawful ending: consciousness refusing absence
with such force that it returns wearing flesh as a garment. The body remains
fixed near the moment of refusal. Breath becomes courtesy. A heartbeat, when
present, is recollection rather than need. Age lays no ordinary claim.

This state cannot sustain its own relationship with the Loom. The aspirant's
thread was cut during the Severance. To remain a person rather than an isolated
will, the vampire must borrow contact through another life.

Thus the hunger.

Blood is the best-known bridge because blood remembers the whole body at once.
It is not the only bridge. Fear carries identity sharply. Memory carries a self
in intimate detail. Some vampires feed upon dreams, devotion, pain, or the
moment a secret leaves one mouth and enters another.

What is consumed matters. How it is taken matters more.

## The Severance

A bite does not, by itself, make a vampire. Neither does blood exchanged beneath
a theatrical moon, burial in unhallowed ground, or the enthusiastic purchase of
a cloak.

The transformation requires three events:

1. **The thread is cut.** The aspirant dies fully enough that ordinary return no
   longer has a path to follow.
2. **The self is retained.** Memory, name, and will remain coherent after every
   bodily proof of identity has ended.
3. **Nonexistence is refused.** The aspirant returns by their own impossible
   insistence.

An elder vampire can prepare the conditions, preserve the name, offer blood as
a road-marker, and call to the aspirant across the silence. The elder cannot
perform the final refusal for them.

For this reason, responsible night households say that vampirism may be guided
but never bestowed. Irresponsible households call the distinction sentimental
and produce more ferals.

Most aspirants fail. Some die. Some return with appetite but without a stable
self. Some hear another will in the silence and open the wrong door.

The survivors are not proof that the rite was wise.

They are proof only that it worked.

## Sunlight and the Unhidden World

Sunlight does not burn vampires because day is holy and night is wicked. Dawn
has sheltered too many crimes for that theology to survive inspection.

Direct sun exposes a vampire to the world's most repeated agreement: morning
follows night; light falls; living bodies belong to time. A vampire is a refusal
of precisely that continuity. Under full daylight, the borrowed bonds holding
flesh and consciousness together begin to show their seams.

The first touch feels like thirst. The second feels like remembering every death
the body denied. Prolonged exposure blackens flesh, scatters memory, and may
reduce even an ancient will to ash and unfinished intention.

Cloud, glass, shade, and heavy cloth offer degrees of protection. None make the
sun false.

## The Courts

Vampire courts are not kingdoms in the ordinary sense. They are disciplines of
appetite, mutual protection, and argument. Age carries influence. Mastery of
hunger carries more.

### The Blood Court

The Blood Court feeds through living bodies. Its oldest covenants regulate
quantity, consent, secrecy, and the treatment of those who provide sustenance.
Some houses maintain paid donor circles and physicians. Others keep captives
behind exquisite legal language.

Blood courtiers are closest to mortal sensation. They are also most vulnerable
to confusing intimacy with ownership.

### The Shadow Court

The Shadow Court feeds on fear: not merely terror, but anticipation, awe, and
the cold clarity of feeling hunted. Its restrained houses work as performers,
confessors, interrogators, or guardians who frighten travelers away from worse
roads. Its cruel houses manufacture helplessness and call the result art.

A Shadow Court maxim says fear freely entered is a door; fear imposed is a cage.
Many courtiers repeat the maxim. Fewer live by it.

### The Memory Court

Memory courtiers drink experience. A willing exchange can remove unbearable
grief for a night, preserve a dying witness, or allow two people to understand
one another with dangerous precision. An unwilling feeding is theft of self.

The Court maintains libraries that no ink could hold: wars remembered by the
afraid, recipes carried in a grandmother's hands, crimes preserved from the
viewpoint of both victim and perpetrator. No scholar agrees whether these
archives are civilization's greatest record or its most elegant violation.

### The Null Court

The Null Court is legendary. Its members are said to have transcended hunger
without ceasing to be vampires. Blood courtiers call them saints. Shadow
courtiers call them liars. Memory courtiers preserve five incompatible accounts
of meeting the same Null adept on the same evening.

If Null vampires exist, no one has proved what sustains them. One theory holds
that they feed upon their own possibility, becoming less real with every year.
Another says they draw connection directly from the Loom and have therefore
ceased to be vampires in all but memory.

The records are unclear.

The envy is not.

## The Covenant of the Necessary Cup

Civil night households recognize some form of the Necessary Cup, though its
clauses vary.

- Take no more than can be survived.
- Do not disguise compulsion as consent.
- Name what is being taken: blood, fear, memory, or another intimacy.
- Leave the giver able to recognize themself.
- Accept responsibility for consequences that outlive the feeding.
- Never make a new vampire merely to excuse an old vampire's loneliness.

The Covenant is not universal law. It has no single court capable of enforcing
it. It remains important because monsters also make rules, and because a rule
broken can become evidence.

The most feared hunters of predatory vampires are often vampires who keep the
Covenant. They know every courteous phrase behind which hunger hides a knife.

## The Five Farewells

_The following section is restricted tradition._

Among covenant-keeping houses, no aspirant approaches the Severance without
completing the **Five Farewells**. They are not tests of obedience. They are five
opportunities to refuse before refusal becomes the only power the aspirant has
left.

### First Farewell: To Innocence

The aspirant is shown the aftermath of ungoverned feeding. In the oldest version,
a farm household lies dead while one adolescent survivor gives testimony. The
aspirant must follow the evidence, identify the hunger responsible, and decide
whether justice means death, restraint, restitution, or a mercy no victim owes.

The lesson is not that all vampires kill.

It is that every vampire can.

### Second Farewell: To Easy Enemies

The aspirant enters a night household thought savage and discovers a disputed
succession: a restrained elder displaced by a claimant who treats slaughter as
strength. Violence may be required. So may negotiation, protection, or the
admission that the supposedly lawful elder helped create the cruelty that
replaced them.

The lesson is that defeating a monster does not prove the person behind it is
good.

### Third Farewell: To Inheritance

The aspirant carries an heirloom to someone descended from, abandoned by, or
otherwise burdened with the elder's mortal life. The recipient may accept the
gift, destroy it, or refuse all contact. The aspirant is forbidden to compel a
different ending.

Some elders use this farewell to repair an old obligation. Some use it to learn
that immortality grants time without granting entitlement.

### Fourth Farewell: To the Sun

The aspirant watches one deliberate sunrise from a place they would grieve never
to see again: a waterfall above the clouds, a winter field turning gold, a
kitchen window, or the face of someone who will not follow them into night.

They remain until full light and leave while leaving is still possible.

No speech is required. Most speeches would make the moment smaller.

### Fifth Farewell: To Certainty

The aspirant prepares a mantle against daylight, settles unfinished promises,
and hears the risks of Severance without romance. At the final threshold, the
elder asks one question: “Do you wish to live, or do you merely fear ending?”

There is no correct answer. There must be an answer.

Then the aspirant is offered another chance to walk away.

The fifth farewell is the only one every ethical elder hopes will be used.

## Mantles of the Borrowed Night

The so-called **suncloak** does not make a vampire immune to daylight. It is a
carefully bound mantle that layers shadow, reflective mineral, old blood, and a
memory of night between wearer and sun. Good mantles buy minutes in open light
and hours beneath cloud or cover. Poor ones fail all at once.

No universal recipe exists. Components must correspond to the wearer: a metal
that remembers shelter, cloth carried through a personal night of terror, an
object willingly given by someone the vampire could have preyed upon and did
not. This is why a mantle cannot simply be purchased from a competent tailor.
The garment is partly an account of restraint.

An elder who promises effortless daylight is selling a costume, a victim, or a
method that transfers the burning elsewhere.

## Death After Death

Destroying a vampire's body does not always end the will that rebuilt it once.
A strongly anchored vampire may reform around preserved blood, a hoarded name,
a memory held by descendants, or the habits of a household that continues
making room at the table.

Reformation is neither quick nor certain. Each return risks losing detail. A
vampire may remember whom they loved but not why, retain hunger without humor,
or awaken convinced that an enemy's account of them is their own.

The surest death is not always fire or sunlight. It is the removal of every
stolen anchor and the willing release of the self that remains.

This is why old vampires fear forgiveness almost as much as oblivion.

## Relations With Other States of Being

New Anteria's law does not classify vampires as Registered Dead. Their souls are
severed, their bodies animate, and their citizenship cases make excellent
careers for patient advocates. Liches consider vampiric return an undisciplined
proof of concept. Many vampires consider lichdom hunger hidden inside
architecture.

The Woven Gods seldom answer vampires directly, whose threads no longer hold in
the ordinary manner. This does not prove divine hatred. The Deep Titans may open
roads to them, but any power willing to bridge such a severance should be asked
what it expects to cross in return.

Memory Court relations with elves are particularly bitter. Both claim duties to
remembrance; each believes the other mistakes possession for stewardship.

## Provenance and Promotion Decisions

This document expands the vampire foundation in
`canon/cultures/races_of_lumia.md` and rewrites the narrative core of the
retired `lore_docs/drafts/cultures/Vampire Race!.md`.

The moral initiation, displaced elder, protected descendant, recovered heirloom,
last sunrise, prepared daylight mantle, and final informed choice are promoted
as the Five Farewells. Borrowed zone names, vnums, level gates, kill lists,
implementation notes, and transformation-token mechanics are retired. The draft
idea that an elder simply “makes” the aspirant a vampire is reconciled with the
established Severance: an elder may guide the return but cannot supply the final
act of will.
