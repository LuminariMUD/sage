# Living Customs of Lumia

- **Spoiler level:** Player-safe

_Collected by Samat Vale, a patient guest and disastrous dancer, from kitchens,
quays, restructuring chambers, memorial offices, and three festivals whose
organizers insist he attended the wrong one._

> A kingdom tells you who has authority.
>
> A homeland tells you what shaped a child.
>
> A custom tells you what people do when no official is watching.

## The Hearth Thesis

Some customs are laws with their armor removed. Others are old accidents that
survived long enough to acquire music.

The cultural historians of Lumia favor a third explanation. They call it the
**Hearth Thesis**: a people repeats certain acts because repetition gives human
scale to forces too large to live beside unnamed. A sealed door makes the
Darkling Wars small enough for one household to remember. A second chair makes
grief small enough to set a place for. A shared cup makes the sea small enough
to cross.

The more mystical version claims every custom strengthens the Loom. This is
comforting and cannot be proved. Customs can preserve cruelty as readily as
kindness. A tradition is not made righteous by age.

It is made powerful by repetition.

## Five Currents, Not Five Peoples

Travel writers once described Lumia as five great cultural spheres. The phrase
was neat, memorable, and wrong. The Five Nations are real political powers; the
Thirteen Homelands are real formative identities. Culture ignores both schemes
whenever a sailor marries inland, a refugee carries a feast across a border, or
a child learns a grandparent's song in a language no census expected.

Five **currents** nevertheless recur across the present age. They overlap. They
quarrel within themselves. No current belongs to one ancestry.

## The Anterean Current: Continuance

Old Anteria fell, but its habits proved more difficult to kill.

Across New Anteria, Hir, the Ubdinas, and scattered diaspora houses, people ask
how a duty continues after the first person carrying it is dead. The Empire's
answer is legal: offices, contracts, and the Registered Dead. Household answers
are less tidy.

### Bells of Resumption

Many Anterean towns ring a low bell at dawn. The custom began in refugee camps,
where a bell meant that enough people had survived the night to begin counting
work. New Anterian clerks now call it the **Bell of Resumption** and attach forms
to it. West Ubdinan villages use a cracked handbell and attach breakfast.

At the fourth division of the working day, memorial offices pause to read names
from the old rolls. Different archives preserve different lists of Old
Anteria's districts. Every archive accuses the others of losing two.

### The Unbroken Chain

At the Feast of Continuance, households pass a chain, cord, or linked wreath
from hand to hand. Imperial ceremony uses gold. Poor districts use onion stems.
The object matters less than the rule: no one may receive it without naming the
person from whom they take responsibility, and no one may pass it without naming
what they refuse to pass on.

The second clause is newer. Traditionalists dislike it intensely, which is one
reason it has spread.

### Diaspora Marks

Anterean communities abroad still place small signs around doors: nail counts,
colored shutters, a floor tile turned against its pattern. Their meanings vary
by city. A mark may promise news, legal witness, shelter for the unregistered
dead, or merely stew without questions. Publishing a complete key is considered
both scholarly vanity and an excellent way to make the key obsolete.

## The Resonant Current: Structure

The Crystal Reaches teach that hidden strain is more dangerous than visible
fracture. Kellustan masons, surface traders, and even foreign musicians have
adopted portions of this principle without sharing Nagburim's caste theology.

### Restructuring

Crystal Dwarves do not sleep as organic people understand sleep. At intervals,
their living lattices quiet while minute stresses settle and their Arcanite
hearts retune the body around them. This **restructuring** may last minutes or
hours. Families often align the beginning or ending of the quiet, allowing the
first shared chord afterward to serve as greeting, health-check, and the simple
relief of hearing loved ones remain themselves.

Disturbing restructuring without necessity is a profound violation. Falsely
declaring necessity is worse.

### The Great Tuning

Once each year, the settlements of the Reaches attempt a common chord. Distance,
weather, politics, and imperfect timing make absolute unity impossible. That is
the point.

The resulting discord is studied for weeks. Orthodoxy calls it a measure of the
people's health. Gilded reformers call it proof that difference can belong to
harmony without being erased. The Transparent Council traditionally publishes
one interpretation. Taverns publish several better ones before noon.

### Fracture Gifts

When a tool, ornament, or body acquires a visible flaw, friends may offer a wire
of gold, silver, or copper. To accept invites help in tracing the damage. To
refuse declares that the fracture will remain unhidden. Both choices are valid
outside the strictest halls.

That last sentence took eighty years and several riots to enter etiquette books.

## The Verdant Current: Succession

In Mosswood, Quechian, and the wandering settlements of Vailand, permanence is
treated as a promise requiring witnesses beyond the present generation.

### The Seasonal Courts

Some forest communities divide civic responsibility among four courts. Spring
speaks for beginnings, Summer for growth, Autumn for harvest and consequence,
Winter for rest, scarcity, and endings. Authority passes with the season. A
Spring judge may answer before a Winter judge months later, and spouses can find
themselves ceremonially superior to one another twice in a year.

No court is simply noble or common. Each holds the kind of decision its season
understands best. Droughts have a habit of proving the categories inadequate.

### The Unnamed Turning

At each seasonal change, certain Quechian communities hold a celebration denied
a permanent name. No performance may be repeated exactly. Old songs are given
new endings. Familiar meals exchange ingredients. Guests arrive by a different
path than the one used last season.

The prohibition is not contempt for memory. It is a promise that remembrance
will not become embalming.

### Patient Houses

Much Verdant architecture is cultivated rather than assembled. A family may
spend years guiding a living house, accepting temporary shelter while root,
branch, and household learn one another. Accelerating the work by magic is not
universally forbidden, but the owner must answer a difficult question: whose
childhood is being shortened for your convenience?

## The Apparatus Current: Chosen Purpose

Arcana Golems inherited designs, commands, and the memory of having been made
for war. They did not inherit a culture capable of telling them what freedom
should look like.

So they are constructing one in public.

### The Workshop Temples

In Apparatus, worship and philosophy take place at benches. Makers construct
objects with no assigned utility: rain-dissolving butterflies, clocks that keep
the time of an absent city, hinges for doors no one intends to build. The work is
called **unpurposed making**.

To create something useless by choice is, for many Arcana Golems, the purest
proof that a maker and a weapon are not the same category.

### The Day of Necessary Error

Perfectly optimized systems tend to preserve the purpose of whoever first
defined perfection. Apparatus therefore observes a recurring day on which safe
processes must admit inefficiency, improvisation, or chance. Citizens take long
routes. Debates assign positions by lot. A flawless public device is given one
harmless asymmetry.

The observance began as philosophy. It became practical after an early council
optimized dissent out of its meeting schedule and required six months to notice.

### Directive Greetings

Among intimate circles, an Arcana Golem may introduce themself by stating a
present purpose: “I am learning patience,” “I am carrying news,” or “I do not
know yet.” Outsiders often mistake the form for an old command protocol. The
difference is essential. A directive received is an instruction. A purpose
declared may be changed.

## The Tide Current: Adaptation

The coasts of Onduis, Axtros, Oorpii, and the Shallow Sea share practices without
sharing a government. **Tide-sworn** is therefore a cultural adjective, not a
nation, charter, or single federation.

### The Shared Cup

A host pours a little water from their cup into a guest's and receives the same
in return. On a ship this promises equal rationing. In a market it promises
honest measure. At a wedding it promises that neither household will remain
unchanged by the joining.

Refusing the exchange is not always hostile. A traveler carrying sickness,
oath-water, or the last water from a lost home may cover the cup and explain.

### Night Markets

Where the tide exposes broad reef or mudflat, temporary markets rise between
one low water and the next. Stalls stand on sledges. Contracts include a clause
for goods reclaimed by the sea. Asking where an object came from is permitted;
believing the first answer is considered a visitor's mistake.

Ashenport's largest night market was outlawed after it began selling permits
authorizing itself. The market now convenes just beyond the quay boundary and
displays the law as its first piece of merchandise.

### Storm Authority

Several maritime communities grant temporary, nearly absolute authority during
crisis. Oorpii's Harbor-Mothers are the best-known example. The deeper custom is
the return: when calm is established, every borrowed privilege must be given
back publicly. A leader reluctant to surrender the storm keys teaches the coast
to distrust the next necessary leader.

## Shared Observances

### The Feast of Sealed Doors

On a winter dark, communities across Lumia close every ordinary entrance from
dusk until dawn. The practice remembers the First Darkling Wars, when terror was
said to enter any threshold left unnamed.

New Antereans press office seals into wax. Quechians guide temporary vines across
doorframes. Crystal Dwarves tune a barrier that rings when touched. Oorpic ships
bind hatch-cords with knots readable from both sides.

No household may refuse a traveler shelter before the sealing. A door closed in
fear is a ward. A door closed against someone freezing is merely a murder that
knows ceremony.

### The Measure at Noon

At the longest day, people measure the shortest shadow they cast. The practice
predates reliable astronomy. A strange length or direction is treated as omen,
joke, or evidence of uneven ground according to local temperament.

The custom does **not** establish the Shadowmoon's orbit or physical nature.
Indeed, Selerish examiners have spent generations demonstrating ordinary causes
for miraculous shadows and have become famous largely because miraculous
shadows keep occurring during their lectures.

### The Festival of Honest Mistakes

Apparatus began it. Ashenport made it loud. Most of Chulan pretends not to
observe it while quietly scheduling peer review for the following morning.

People display a consequential error from the past year and name what it taught
them. The person who presents the most instructive failure receives a crooked
crown and no authority whatsoever. This final provision is the result of an
earlier, more educational version of the festival.

## Goods That Carry Culture

### Dreamwood

Cultivated near thin Feyveil, dreamwood grows in the waking world and the
Otherworld at once. Its grain may alter according to the sleeper nearest it.
Harvest requires agreement from stewards on both sides, a statement easier to
write than fulfill.

### Memory Silk

Anterean looms produce cloth trained to retain fold, pressure, and occasionally
strong emotion. It makes superb archival wrapping and dangerous formal wear.
Diplomats have learned never to send a memory-silk coat to an ordinary laundry.

### Calculated Steel

Apparatus forges tune repeated pieces to minute tolerances, then introduce one
documented irregularity so the series remains adaptable. Foreign buyers pay
extra for flawless sets. Apparatus makers charge extra to refuse.

### Tide Pearls

Rare Shallow Sea oysters form layered pearls after magical tides, planar leaks,
or other disturbances. Navigators read the layers as records of water under
stress. Claims that tide pearls form only beneath the Shadowmoon are profitable,
popular, and unsupported.

## The Three Long Arguments

### Permanence

Crystal orthodoxy sees endurance as truth made visible. Verdant practice sees
unending form as a refusal to nourish what comes next. Their arguments meet at
bridges, roads, and border halls—structures one people wants tuned for centuries
and another wants capable of graceful return to soil.

### Memory

Anterean offices preserve records. Elves share remembrance. Halflings document
what larger powers overlook. Sanctine refugees maintain pauses for people who
cannot answer. All value memory. None agree on who owns it, whether it may be
corrected, or when mercy requires forgetting.

### The Soul

Arcana Golems ask whether a made consciousness can inherit, worship, marry, and
refuse its maker. Liches, Registered Dead, vampires, and Crystal Dwarves make
every easy answer unusable. Courts still seek a universal definition. Ordinary
people continue forming families faster than the rulings arrive.

## Language and Belonging

The once-popular **Three Tongue Theory**—Trade Tongue, Heart Tongue, and a lost
True Tongue—survives as philosophy. Only the first two are public linguistic
categories. Common is Lumia's Trade Tongue. The twelve canonical Heart Tongues
and their Homeland grants are established in
`canon/cultures/languages_of_the_homelands.md`.

No culture is biologically restricted to a language, greeting, feast, or mode of
life. Customs travel in people. People contradict their customs. Both facts are
signs that a culture remains alive.

## Provenance and Promotion Decisions

This document rewrites the durable customs in the retired
`lore_docs/drafts/cultures/CULTURES_OF_LUMIA.md` and reconciles them with the
Five Nations, Thirteen Homelands, Heart Tongues, Crystal Dwarf canon, and Arcana
Golem origins.

Continuance rites, restructuring, seasonal courts, unpurposed making, necessary
error, maritime exchange, shared observances, signature goods, and the three
long cultural arguments are promoted. The claim that five cultural spheres map
cleanly onto Lumia's peoples, exact biological cultural rules, fixed salt
currency, and contradicted language grants are retired.
