# Relics and the Art of Making

- **Canon status:** [ESTABLISHED]
- **Canon version:** `relics-making-1.0.0`
- **Approved use:** Item lore, crafting traditions, artifact stories, treasure
  placement, and non-numeric reward design
- **Spoiler level:** Player-safe, except where marked restricted

_A concordance attributed to Ivenna Coalbright, who maintained that a hammer
should be named only after it had struck the maker's thumb and learned remorse._

> Every treasure tells three stories: the hands that made it, the hands that
> lost it, and the hands it has not met yet.

## What an Object Keeps

The common mistake is to divide the world into enchanted things and ordinary
things.

There are no ordinary things. There are only things whose memories speak softly.

A quay-token keeps the salt of every palm that worried its edge. A soldier's
plain knife remembers being sharpened before a battle and left dull after a
funeral. A crown remembers the shape of command even when it lies upside down in
mud. Most such memories never rise beyond sentiment. Some are given voice by
craft. A very few acquire enough name, purpose, and possibility to answer back.

Makers describe these three weights as:

- **Memory:** what the object has carried and what its bearers refuse to forget;
- **Purpose:** what the object has been made, trained, or persuaded to do;
- **Possibility:** what future begins leaning toward anyone who takes it up.

An object with only purpose is a tool. An object with purpose and memory may
become a relic. When all three weights pull hard enough to bend a life around
them, scholars begin using the dangerous word **artifact**.

## Four Degrees of Wonder

The degrees below describe narrative weight, not price or a wielder's prowess.
A kitchen spoon can become a relic. A jeweled blade can remain merely expensive.

### The Well-Made

These are honest tools whose excellence is still entirely the work of material,
training, and care. Ashenport rope knives, Oorpic storm cord, Kellustan bridge
hammers, and Quechian seed cages belong here. Their makers know that reliability
is a quiet magic even when no spell is involved.

### The Awakened

An awakened object carries a stable working or a responsive memory. A cloak may
grow warm before the rain it has learned to recognize. A ring may tighten when a
particular oath is endangered. Such things possess habit, not necessarily mind.
Treating habit as loyalty is how many collections acquire curses.

### The Named Relic

A relic has become inseparable from an account larger than one owner. Its name
survives transfer. Its past limits and enlarges what it can do. The blade that
defended a gate for three generations may refuse assassination, not because the
blade is moral, but because murder in a bedroom does not resemble the purpose it
remembers.

### The Artifact

An artifact is not merely a stronger relic. It is a local argument with reality.
It may preserve an impossible condition, carry a will, or make the world repeat
a story around it. Every dependable artifact is singular. A convincing copy may
share appearance or even power; it cannot share the exact knot of Memory, Will,
and Possibility that made the first one itself.

The wise do not ask how much an artifact can do.

They ask what it is trying to continue.

## The Three Schools of Making

The names below are used across Lumia because traders require labels. No culture
practices any school in precisely the same manner.

### The Forge School

Forge-makers transform through pressure, heat, rhythm, and declared intent.
Metal is their most familiar language, but kiln, glass, fired clay, and worked
stone follow related disciplines.

The Ember Throne says every hammerfall should carry one honest thought. A blade
forged while its maker pretends not to hate will keep the hatred and forget the
pretense. Ashenport guilds therefore place a witness beside important work, not
to prevent theft but to ask, at intervals, “What are you making now?”

Forge craft excels at fixing purpose. It also risks fixing a passing emotion as
though it were a lifelong truth.

### The Growth School

Growth-makers guide a material toward a form latent within it. Crystal
resonators cultivate lattices by tone. Quechian woodworkers negotiate with root
and grain. Certain elven houses coax dreamwood through both sides of the
Feyveil, accepting that a chair grown in two realms may remember two occupants.

Time is not delay in this school. Time is an ingredient.

Forced growth produces quick wonders with shallow memory. Patient growth can
make an object that continues adapting after the maker's death. Crystal Dwarf
artisans warn that guidance and control sound alike to anyone who has never been
allowed to refuse.

### The Binding School

Binding joins an existing pattern to a prepared vessel: spell to ring, oath to
blade, memory to cloth, or soul to constructed body. Arcana Golem making is its
most consequential expression.

Every binding requires an agreement, but agreement and consent are not synonyms.
A coerced soul can be fastened. A dying memory can be stolen. An unwilling oath
can be nailed into iron. Such work may function perfectly and remain an atrocity.
The wrongness does not always appear as weakness. Often it appears as obedience
so complete that the vessel spends centuries learning how to hate.

Ethical binders require the terms to name:

- what enters the vessel;
- what the vessel may change;
- what ends the bond;
- who bears the cost of release;
- and whether the bound presence may say no after learning what yes meant.

The last clause was added after the Last War. It was written in several million
deaths.

## Arcanite Work

Arcanite can retain magical pattern with an intimacy few materials survive. It
is focus, vessel, amplifier, and—when used carelessly—an invitation for every
working it remembers.

Makers commonly describe five stages of Arcanite craft. These are not measures
of power and do not guarantee that an object will advance from one to the next.

1. **Preparation:** The material is cleaned of conflicting purpose and given
   channels through which resonance can travel.
2. **Infusion:** Arcanite dust, shard, or tuned contact teaches the object to
   hold a working.
3. **Awakening:** Repeated use gives the object preferences, habits, or a
   recognizable response to its bearer.
4. **Transfiguration:** The object begins retaining a form or function that
   mundane material alone could not sustain.
5. **Apotheosis:** Name, history, will, and possibility converge until the
   object no longer depends entirely upon its original matter.

The fifth stage cannot be scheduled. Anyone selling a guaranteed apotheosis is
offering fraud, sacrifice, or both.

Every use of Arcanite also belongs to the wider crisis. The crystal is Aeonic
residue and part of the substance by which the Prison is maintained. A brilliant
focus can be simultaneously a masterpiece, a Crystal Dwarf's lost future, and
a grain taken from a cosmic wall. Craft does not cease to be beautiful because
its material is compromised. Beauty simply acquires an account book.

## Storied Artifacts

Not every artifact is made by belief, but some become real in the form the world
has repeated for them. Lorekeepers call these **storied artifacts**.

They possess three faces:

1. **The Tale:** what is said around fires, in courts, and by people who want the
   object to mean something;
2. **The Truth:** the event, craft, theft, accident, or sacrifice beneath the
   telling;
3. **The Hunger:** the condition the object requires in order to remain itself.

The Hunger is not always literal appetite. It may be a deed repeated, a question
answered, an oath kept, a place revisited, or a kind of bearer chosen. When the
Hunger goes unmet, an artifact may sleep, sour, leave, or turn its purpose upon
the person withholding it.

This is the lore beneath the old phrase **artifact possession**. The bearer does
not simply possess the artifact. For part of each day, the grammar runs the other
way.

## How Artifacts Change Hands

Three patterns recur often enough to deserve names.

### The Trial

An artifact rests behind a guardian, riddle, impossible courtesy, or moral
choice that resembles its own history. Killing the guardian may win the room
and fail the trial. Answering the riddle may reveal that the correct answer is a
person willing to leave the object where it lies.

### The Bestowal

A god, Titan, elder power, or present bearer may grant a relic after service,
exchange, or sacrifice. A bestowed artifact carries the giver's interpretation
of the bargain. Wise recipients ask whether the object agreed.

### The Taking

Artifacts are stolen, inherited, looted, won, and found in the pockets of coats
that did not contain them the previous morning. Taking can transfer custody. It
does not necessarily transfer recognition. A relic that refuses its new bearer
may become inert. An artifact with a stronger opinion may make the theft the
first chapter of a punishment.

No manner of acquisition makes violence righteous by itself. “I wanted what
they carried” is motive, not moral authority.

## The Strife of Singular Things

True artifacts resist sharing a single fate.

When one bearer attempts to keep two, their possibilities pull against one
another. Mild Strife appears as heat, sleeplessness, contradictory omens, or the
objects vanishing in turns. Severe Strife can fracture memory, kill everyone in
a room, or erase both artifacts from the present until their stories find safer
hands.

This is the truth beneath the vulgar warning that anyone holding two artifacts
will explode. The warning is not reliably literal.

It has been literal often enough.

## Three Artifacts in Disputed Record

### The Sword of Seventeen Sorrows

**The tale:** Seventeen widows placed one tear each into a quenching trough, and
the smith drew from that water a blade with seventeen mercies.

**The likely truth:** An apprentice forged an excellent but ordinary sword after
a battle and gave each bereaved household a different account of its making.
The stories met years later. Instead of canceling one another, they braided.

**The hunger:** The sword must be permitted to witness grief without being used
to hurry grief toward vengeance.

Each sorrow can lend the bearer a different strength. Each also returns a memory
of loss. The sword has abandoned wielders who wanted power but declined mourning.

### The Crown of the Unfinished Proof

**The tale:** A Crystal Dwarf resonator calculated the final shape of justice and
set the answer into a crown rather than impose it upon the world.

**The likely truth:** The crown is surface work, grown from a discarded Nagburim
practice shard by three philosophers who could agree on no government except
one that remained revisable.

**The hunger:** It must receive a proposition its bearer sincerely cannot solve.
Feed it only easy certainties and it begins simplifying the wearer instead.

The Crown reveals branching consequences with painful clarity. It never reveals
which consequence is right.

### The Quiet Throne

**The tale:** Halfling makers built a chair that could hide a good ruler from
fate. Fate overlooked the ruler, then the room, then most of the eastern wing.

**The likely truth:** It was intended to make the overlooked impossible to
ignore. The binding reversed when every patron tried to place their own name at
its center.

**The hunger:** Someone of consequence must sit without title, herald, or
witness and willingly endure being unimportant.

The Throne can make a seated figure nearly impossible to notice. Each use risks
loosening one memory by which the sitter knows who they are.

## Legacy Making

An item can outlive its first purpose without becoming empty.

When a beloved adventurer retires, communities sometimes name the tool most
closely associated with their story: a chipped cup, a plain spear, the coat that
survived six shipwrecks and one marriage. The bearer gives it away, lays it in a
public keeping-place, or asks that it continue traveling.

Such **legacy pieces** are not manufactured honors. They awaken only if other
hands take up both object and obligation. A famous sword displayed behind glass
may remain steel. A fisher's patched net used for another century may learn to
draw drowning sailors from water it has never touched.

This is how mortal memory enters craft without requiring a throne, a god, or an
official decree. The world remembers what people continue doing for one another.

## Restricted Corrections

- The Five Locks are **Memory, Identity, Time, Space, and Will**. They are not a
  tier of collectible artifacts. Material keys, shards, or instruments may be
  claimed in stories to resonate with a Lock, but no such claim establishes the
  Lock's location or reduces it to an object.
- Arcanite is not “crystallized possibility” created by contact with the Primal
  Realm. Its restricted origin is Aeonic residue, as established in
  `canon/timeline/the_shattered_truth.md`.
- No artifact cycle has a fixed calendar. Some artifacts exhibit narrative
  migration, but claims that all of them rotate on a regular schedule are
  collectors' folklore.
- Crafting traditions do not establish numeric enhancement tiers, equipment-slot
  percentages, level bands, or market formulae.
