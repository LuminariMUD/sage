# The Feyveil and the Otherworld

- **Canon status:** [ESTABLISHED]
- **Canon version:** `feyveil-otherworld-1.0.0`
- **Approved use:** Planar lore, elven tradition, thin-place locations, fey
  encounters, and Otherworld travel design
- **Spoiler level:** Player-safe through “Thin Places”; restricted thereafter

_A field guide begun by Osric Pell, who returned from the Otherworld with every
fact intact and no remaining belief that facts should stand in that order._

> In Lumia, a rose is a flower that may symbolize grief.
>
> Beyond the Feyveil, grief may bloom red and ask why you cut it.

## The Nearer Elsewhere

The **Otherworld** is a parallel reach of reality where definition has substance.
Thought, emotion, promise, resemblance, and metaphor are not merely qualities
laid over matter. They can be handled, traded, wounded, planted, and followed
home.

A locked door in Lumia bars passage. An Otherworld door may be locked because
the traveler is keeping a secret, and no key will turn until the secret changes
hands. A winter there may be weather, mourning, a queen, or all three wearing
one white face.

This does not mean the Otherworld is a dream in which anything imagined becomes
true. It has inhabitants, histories, hungers, and rules older than a visitor's
desire. Thought is tangible there. It is not sovereign.

The realm lies near Lumia without lying at a measurable distance. Scholars call
the boundary between them the **Feyveil**.

## The Feyveil

The Feyveil is not a wall. It is the world's habit of keeping thing and meaning
from becoming the same object.

Where the Veil is strong, sorrow remains inside a mourner, names remain sounds,
and a path goes where the ground permits. Where it thins, emotion acquires
weather, an old name opens a young door, and roads remember destinations absent
from the map.

Powerful workings can press the Veil open or stitch it closed. Sudden magical
discharge may tear it without intention. Neither act is exact. A sealed breach
can force pressure toward another place. An opened passage may admit only
qualities rather than bodies: a district gains an Otherworld autumn, everyone
inside dreams the same fox, or every promise spoken there becomes visible thread
until dawn.

## Thin Places

No complete atlas of thin places remains correct for long. The best-known kinds
include:

- **twilight pools**, whose surfaces reflect a sky one moment ahead of the
  present;
- **root hollows**, where old trees have grown around definitions such as home,
  hunger, or shelter;
- **repeated rooms**, including the Dollhouse on the Ashenport Road, where one
  memory has made architecture of itself;
- **dream crossings**, opened by collective sleep, elven remembrance, or a
  power answering from the other side;
- **aftermath wounds**, where catastrophic magic left thing and meaning unable
  to separate cleanly.

Travelers should distrust any guide who guarantees which form a breach will
take. A pool may be a road one year and merely deep water the next.

## The Pools of Twilight

Elven traditions remember the **Pools of Twilight** as the passages through
which their earliest ancestors entered Lumia. The exact locations remain closely
guarded and are not established by public canon.

Three versions of the crossing survive.

### The Joined Crossing

In this account, fey beings desired the solidity of mortal life but could not
cross individually. They joined purpose into one great presence, pierced the
Feyveil, and spent nearly all of that power in the passage. On Lumia's side they
separated again as the first elves: physically mature, naked, wide-eyed, and
without practiced memory of how to be mortal.

They learned quickly because the Otherworld remained in them as dream and
instinct.

### The Dream That Woke

Another tradition says the Otherworld dreams, and elves are dreams that refused
to end. They stepped from the Pools already old in memory, carrying recollections
of forests that had never existed in Lumia.

This is the account most commonly taught in elven poetry and in
`canon/cultures/races_of_lumia.md`.

### The Mortal Editing

A late and controversial theory claims the Luminari altered the first crossing
souls, making them capable of returning through repeated lives. No approved
record proves such intervention. The theory may preserve contact between elves
and wayward Luminari during the Age of Vigil; it may also be a mortal attempt to
make reincarnation sound engineered and therefore controllable.

The accounts disagree about the travelers' memory. They agree on desire: the
first elves chose weight, consequence, and the risk of ending.

## The Returning

Elder elves may undertake a private rite of return at a Twilight Pool. They
enter and do not emerge by the same road.

Some elven houses teach that the traveler dissolves into the Otherworld and may
return centuries later with another face. Others distinguish this chosen return
from the natural **Fading**, through which an elf gradually becomes more memory
than matter. Still others say both beliefs soften a final death no witness can
follow.

There is no dependable test for continuity. An elf may return carrying an old
name, a stranger's recollection, or a grief that recognizes a family none of
their features resemble.

The ceremony is reserved for those judged ready. Entering a Pool unprepared can
strip solidity without preserving a coherent self. The crossing feels, according
to one survivor, like walking through deepening syrup while every remembered
version of oneself argues over which body deserves to reach the far bank.

## Speech and Thought Beyond the Veil

In the Otherworld, names attract the things they describe. Metaphor gives
directions. Silence can be a physical shelter if entered honestly.

Telepathic contact becomes easier because thought is nearer the surface, but
privacy does not disappear. It becomes craft. Experienced travelers carry
**closed images**—memories or invented scenes folded around more vulnerable
thoughts. Fey hosts consider crude mental walls the equivalent of shouting with
one's hands over one's ears.

Language still matters. A promise made in Common may bind its practical terms. A
promise made in a Heart Tongue can carry the cultural meaning of obligation. A
promise made in an Otherworld name may decide what the speaker becomes in order
to keep it.

## The Root-Cities of Quechian

In western Quechian, enormous darkwood trees create vertical worlds. Their
canopies catch weather and wandering fey. Their inhabited trunks hold bridges,
shrines, and seasonal homes. Their roots split bedrock into caverns warm with
sap, fungus, and light that has forgotten the sun.

Communities below cultivate root fungi and collect sap only from channels the
trees have released. The sap ferments into a drink called **deep amber**, prized
for dreams in which the drinker remembers being architecture. Overharvesting
causes upper leaves to shed memories rather than color, a harm the Covenant of
Shade treats as both ecological damage and assault.

The greatest of these trees is the **Giant Darkwood Tree** named in Quechian
road lore. Some roots cross the Feyveil entirely. No explorer has mapped the
lowest chambers twice in the same order.

## Swiftpaths and the Otherworld

One enduring theory says the Swiftpaths cross the Otherworld to evade ordinary
distance. It would explain their attention to intention and the way some
travelers arrive at the destination they need rather than the one they named.

Other evidence favors travel through the Loom or direct alteration of spatial
relationship. None of the theories is established as sole truth. The approved
Swiftpath concordance remains `canon/locations/the_swiftpaths.md`.

Travelers should not assume that every instantaneous journey enters the
Otherworld, nor that every Otherworld path moves quickly. One expedition spent
an afternoon crossing a bluebell field and returned after the field had
experienced forty-three winters. Lumia had experienced six minutes and one
unusually specific frost.

## Shadowmoon Weather

During Shadowmoon approaches, distinctions weaken. Thin places may broaden,
overlap, or carry collective memories across the Feyveil. This effect is
consistent with established accounts of planar barriers loosening.

It does not establish that the Shadowmoon is a conventional moon, a planar
object, an egg, an eye, or a probability event. Those are interpretations. The
crossings are weather.

## Five Courtesies for Crossing

1. **Carry your full name, but do not offer all of it.** A name is identity and
   invitation.
2. **Do not eat a symbol you cannot explain.** Pomegranate, grief, hospitality,
   and sunset may share a plate.
3. **Ask whether a path is going somewhere.** Some paths are resting. Some are
   hunting.
4. **Pay attention to tense.** An Otherworld host promising that you _were_ safe
   may be perfectly honest.
5. **Bring back fewer promises than you carried in.** New obligations cross the
   Veil more easily than people do.

## Restricted Concordance

- The Otherworld is a parallel realm in which concepts and definitions can
  become tangible.
- The Feyveil separates and overlaps it with Lumia.
- The Pools of Twilight are tied to elven origin and return traditions, but
  their locations and exact mechanism remain unresolved.
- Otherworld influence explains neither every fey event nor every Swiftpath.
- No canon establishes universal reincarnation mechanics for elves or proves
  Luminari alteration of elven souls.
