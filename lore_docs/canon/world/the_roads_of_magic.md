> A spell is a door described so precisely that the world briefly mistakes it

## One Grammar, Several Roads

The first error taught to young mages is that magic is one thing.

The second is that it is many.

Every working known in Lumia uses a related grammar: attention, address,
shaping, and release. A spell must notice what is present. It must reach toward
what is absent. It must give the arriving power a form. At last, if the caster
is wise and the day is kind, it must let that power go.

The Luminari taught this grammar. Gods answer through it. Titans permit their
servants to draw through it. Arcanists use it to call the first elements near.
Druids hide it in seed-songs and burial loam. A psion may perform all four
movements between one heartbeat and the next without speaking a word.

Shared grammar does not mean shared source. Two letters may use the same
alphabet and still arrive from opposite sides of the world.

This is the narrow foundation on which the rival colleges agree:

- **Arcane magic** calls ungoverned power from the Primal Reach and shapes it
  by mortal learning and will.
- **Woven divine magic** travels through relationship with a god anchored in
  the Loom.
- **Deep divine magic** crosses an opened way from a Titan's dominion.
- **Primal practice** awakens the memory already held by life, land, and the
  permeable edge of the Feyveil.
- **Psionic practice** makes will itself the instrument and risks the self in
  place of an outside medium.

These are roads, not boxes. Storm-singers, blood mathematicians, moon druids,
and the Knights of the Shattered Mirror have all embarrassed taxonomists.
Nevertheless, a map with uncertain borders is better than calling every
horizon the same country.

## Why Magic Is Possible

The forbidden archives offer a simple and terrible answer: magic exists because
reality is separated from itself.

Will is not the world, and so will can reach toward the world. Memory is not the
present, and so memory can be carried into it. Possibility has not happened, and
so a spell can invite one future nearer than its neighbors. The Loom of Aether
holds these distances in tension. Magic is the practiced act of crossing them.

This does not make the Loom a reservoir from which a caster ladles power. It is
the architecture that permits a call to travel, a form to endure, and a cost to
find its owner. Memory gives a working continuity. Will gives it direction.
Possibility gives it somewhere to arrive.

No road escapes all three.

## The Arcane Road

### The Primal Reach

The oldest mortal lessons name the source of arcane power the **Primal Realm**.
Modern arcanists often prefer **Primal Reach**, because _realm_ suggests a place
one might visit with boots, provisions, and a sufficiently optimistic map.

No dependable expedition has proved the matter so tidy.

The Reach is where elemental forms are least divided: flame before hearth or
wildfire, lightning before storm or nerve, living pattern before flesh chooses
fur, scale, leaf, or skin. Some schools call it a plane. Some call it the first
memory of matter. Dragon philosophers insist it is simply the world before the
world learned restraint.

Whatever its nature, the call is real.

The Luminari first taught dragons to hear it through the possibility carried in
their blood. Later they gave simpler calls—cantrips, sparks, shaped breaths—to
the peoples of Lumia. In time, mortal students learned to open the road without
dragon blood or Luminari guidance. That discovery changed civilization. It also
made the Last War possible.

### The Four Movements of a Calling

Arcanists describe a complete working in four movements.

1. **Attention:** The caster apprehends the present condition without the
   comfort of pretending it is simpler than it is.
2. **Address:** The caster opens a correspondence with a pattern in the Primal
   Reach.
3. **Shaping:** Memory and disciplined will give that pattern a local form.
4. **Release:** The road is closed and the summoned form is allowed to belong
   to the world—or to depart it.

Failure in attention makes a spell answer the wrong problem. Failure in address
calls nothing, or something adjacent to nothing. Failure in shaping gives raw
power no courtesy of purpose. Failure in release leaves a door believing it is
still needed.

This is why the finest arcanists spend more time ending spells than beginning
them.

### What Arcane Magic Can Shape

Arcane practice is broad because the Primal Reach is broad. A caller may draw
raw element, motion, pressure, light, or a pattern complex enough to imitate
living tissue. Arcane healing is therefore possible, but it is reconstruction,
not mercy. The spell must know what the body remembers itself to be. Where that
memory is absent, divided, or false, the restored flesh may be flawless and
still belong to the wrong history.

The same principle governs augmentation. Magic can persuade muscle to remember
greater strength or bone to accept a harder possibility. The body eventually
answers with its own opinion. Wise practitioners listen before the disagreement
becomes permanent.

### The Arcane Price

Arcane power has no patron to judge the petition before it arrives. This is its
freedom and its danger.

- **Attention has a limit.** Fatigue, fear, pain, and divided purpose make the
  address imprecise.
- **Form resists interruption.** Armor, burdens, and constrained movement do
  not make arcane magic impossible, but many callings depend on breath, gesture,
  balance, and uninterrupted thought. The Shattered Mirror trains specifically
  to survive this difficulty.
- **The called leaves residue.** Repeated fire workings make a place eager for
  flame. Repeated concealments teach rooms to keep secrets from their owners.
- **The caster is also shaped.** A mind that spends decades treating reality as
  negotiable may forget that other wills are not material.

The popular saying that arcane magic is “power without permission” is only half
true. It requires no divine permission. The world still sends an invoice.

## The Two Divine Roads

The full distinction between Woven Gods and Deep Titans is established in
`canon/factions/THE_TWO_ORDERS_OF_DIVINITY.md`. No magical college may collapse
that distinction merely because the miracles resemble one another.

### The Woven Road

A devotee knots name, memory, practice, and oath into the Loom. A Woven God
shapes power through that relationship and returns the miracle along it. The
deity is not a distant reservoir. It is author, witness, and participant.

The price is therefore relational. A broken vow wounds both channel and anchor.
A forgotten name narrows the road. A god may refuse a prayer, misunderstand it,
or answer the need beneath it rather than the request placed into words.

Knightly miracles belong to this road. A knight's oath is not payment after the
fact. It is the bridge on which the answer travels.

### The Deep Road

A servant of a Titan reaches toward the patron's pocket dominion. The Titan
opens—or declines to open—a way and shapes what crosses it. Healing often comes
readily because a mind vaster than the supplicant prepares the pattern before it
arrives. Power alien to the Titan's dominion may come slowly or not at all.

The price is nearness. Every successful petition wears the road wider. A mortal
who draws too deeply may become Titan Blessed: first a favored channel, then a
door that cannot close, and at last an avatar who must ask whether the voice
speaking through their mouth is still their own.

Gods ask to be remembered. Titans do not need remembrance, but attention brings
them close. Neither bargain is free.

## The Primal Practice

Arcanists reach outward for first forms. Primal practitioners listen downward
for forms that have already learned to live.

A forest remembers rain long after the sky has cleared. A hunting path remembers
fear. A river remembers every bank that tried to contain it. Through root,
weather, bone, migration, and the thin places of the Feyveil, druids and shamans
invite those memories to act in the present.

Primal workings are reciprocal. One does not command a grove without becoming
part of what the grove must carry. A healer may take a fever into winter soil
and find spring growth sickly in return. A shape-walker who borrows the wolf too
often may discover that doors have begun to look like traps.

The practice is strongest where relationship is old and weakest where land has
been stripped of memory. This is why Mosswood's rites travel poorly and why a
Quechian druid asks a place its name before offering help.

## The Psionic Practice

Psionic discipline makes no ordinary external call. It places trained will
against an accepted limit and declines the limit.

Distance says _far_; the psion answers _near_. Pain says _obey_; the psion
answers _information_. A hostile mind says _mine_; the psion must answer without
forgetting that another self is not an empty room.

Because will is both hand and tool, the psionic price falls upon identity.
Excessive practice can produce echo-thoughts, borrowed emotion, false memory, or
the conviction that everything which can be overruled ought to be. Trelux
contact is especially dangerous: a mortal will meeting their collective may
return expanded, erased, or unable to tell the difference.

Changelings and Arcana Golems often show unusual talent for psionic work. One is
never finished becoming; the other already exists in defiance of an intended
purpose. Neither fact makes the practice safe.

## Arcanite: Bridge, Vessel, and Bar

Arcanite is not frozen arcane spellwork, nor a common mineral made potent by the
Primal Reach. The restricted histories identify it as crystallized residue of
the shattered Aeon from which the Luminari came.

That origin explains its breadth. Arcanite can hold a pattern, amplify an
address, store a working, and stabilize a form that ordinary matter would
reject. Luminari techniques taught mortals to grow, cut, and tune it so expertly
that later traditions mistook refinement for creation.

Arcanite is therefore useful along every road without being the source of every
road. An arcanist uses it as focus and memory. A temple may use it to preserve a
litany's cadence. A Titan cult may use it to brace a narrow gate. Crystal Dwarves
carry living lattices of it at their hearts.

It is also the substance of the Prison.

Every extraction is consequently both craft and trespass. Not every chip taken
from Lumia opens a visible crack, and no honest scholar can calculate a simple
exchange between weight mined and danger caused. The connection remains
canonical: depletion weakens the Prison, threatens Crystal Dwarf survival, and
makes civilization increasingly dependent upon the thing it cannot safely
consume.

## Crossing Roads

It is possible to learn more than one practice. It is not possible to live more
than one life without dividing one's attention among them.

The dual-trained caller gains breadth and loses the ease of a single answer. An
arcanist taught divine petition must distinguish personal will from patronal
intent. A druid learning psionic refusal must know when a landscape is speaking
and when desire is wearing the landscape's voice. A god-sworn knight who opens a
Titan's road has not merely learned a second spell. They have invited two vast
wills to meet inside one mortal promise.

Some achieve astonishing synthesis. More become fluent in beginnings and poor
at endings.

## Magic Beneath the Shadowmoon

The Shadowmoon's accepted effect is not greater power but weakened distinction.
The boundary between individual and collective loosens. So do neighboring
boundaries: memory and present sensation, caster and working, one opened road
and another.

During an approach, spells may carry a stranger's recollection. Primal rites may
answer in a chorus. Divine petitions may be overheard by powers not addressed.
Psionic contact may persist after both minds believe it ended. Arcane residue
becomes particularly eager to repeat itself.

No canonical account establishes the Shadowmoon's body, orbit, or ultimate
origin. Magical doctrine records the weather it causes and leaves the moon to
astronomers, prophets, and liars.

## The Five Cautions

Every reputable school teaches some version of these cautions:

1. **Name the road before opening it.** A familiar effect does not prove a
   familiar source.
2. **Know what is doing the shaping.** Power shaped by a patron carries the
   patron's understanding; power shaped by the caster carries the caster's
   blindness.
3. **Close what you call.** An unfinished ending is an invitation written in
   the future tense.
4. **Account for the witness.** Places, bodies, gods, and tools remember being
   used.
5. **Pay the cost while it is still yours to pay.** Deferred prices acquire
   interest and descendants.

## Restricted Concordance

The following claims are established for custodians of secret lore:

- Magic is possible because separation makes room between will and reality.
- Arcanite is crystallized Aeonic essence and a structural substance of the
  Prison.
- The common magical grammar descends from Luminari instruction.
- The Primal Reach is the accepted source named by the oldest arcane account;
  whether it is a place, condition, or first memory remains unresolved.
- Woven and Deep miracles must retain their distinct sources.
- No tradition has demonstrated power without consequence.

This concordance does not establish spell lists, armor percentages, resource
costs, numeric schools, or the Shadowmoon's physical behavior.
