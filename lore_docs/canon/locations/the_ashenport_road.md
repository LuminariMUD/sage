# The Ashenport Road

- **Canon status:** [ESTABLISHED ADVENTURE FRAMEWORK]
- **Canon version:** `ashenport-road-1.0.0`
- **Approved use:** Ashenport regional quests, named NPCs, local dungeons,
  Darkling investigations, relic placement, and progressive campaign structure
- **Spoiler level:** Player-safe through “The Lenadrian Family”; restricted
  thereafter

_Compiled by Veralis Sorn from Althair Lenadrian's field books, Ulerion's last
route marks, statements collected at the Jade Jug, and corrections left in a
handwriting which may belong to Serane and may belong to a talented forger._

> A road is a story that has agreed to lie still.
>
> The Ashenport Road has never been especially agreeable.

## The Road and Its City

Ashenport stands at the mouth of the River Veyr within Onduis, a charter city of
the Free Cities of Kohn. Its Republic of Nine bargains for the waters and lands
beyond the walls while its mayor and judges keep the streets. Its full public
identity is established in `canon/locations/the_thirteen_homelands.md`.

The **Ashenport Road** is not one paved line. It is the web of dangerous routes,
old ruins, hidden thresholds, and Swiftpath journeys connected to the Lenadrian
investigation into returning Darkling activity. It begins in a Mosswood border
settlement, passes through the Great Port, and repeatedly leaves the safety of
maps.

Local tellers call the connected events **the Lenadrian Inheritance**.

The inheritance is not land.

It is a question no member of the family managed to put down.

## The Lenadrian Family

### Althair Lenadrian, Who Looked Too Long

Althair began as a mercenary on Ashenport's roads and became an explorer after
finding a cave whose walls showed figures of smoke, oil-dark limbs, and a bright
woman sealing a broken sphere.

He spent the wealth of a long career on ruins, pre-war testimony, and objects
that made compasses ashamed. His later expeditions concerned the Luminari, then
still dismissed in educated circles as several myths sharing a name. One
expedition returned without most of its members. Those who survived sickened,
dreamed the same dream, and died in different cities on the same night.

Althair returned speaking of Darklings and five kinds of absence. Ashenport
called him mad because madness was cheaper than closing the eastern road.

He may also have been mad.

Truth and injury often occupy the same house.

### Cecilia Astorian Lenadrian, Who Catalogued the Impossible

Cecilia was the youngest daughter of an Ashenport antiquarian and the first
person able to read Althair's field index without becoming angry. She catalogued
his early finds, corrected his invented provenance, and required every object in
their house to carry a note naming what they did not know.

She died after a long wasting illness when her sons were young. Althair suspected
an artifact carried home from his travels. No evidence proves him right. The
possibility made caution into obsession and left the sons to be raised among
servants, arguments, and rooms their father kept locked.

### Alerion, Who Left and Remained

Alerion was the elder son. He trained for a time under a civic oath-house allied
with the Crimson Loom, then abandoned the path after concluding that his father's
warnings were grief wearing scholarship.

He has regretted the conclusion more faithfully than he ever kept an oath.

In the present age he works from the **Tavern of the Jade Jug**, maintaining a
network of road wardens, craftspeople, smugglers, scholars, and people too
sensible to place their names in an archive. He drinks more than Veralis approves
and less than Ashenport gossip requires. On difficult evenings, he turns his
glass a quarter-circle between questions, as his mother once turned disputed
artifacts on her cataloguing cloth.

Alerion believes his father's work must be finished. His greatest danger is not
drink or despair.

It is the belief that only he can finish it.

### Ulerion, Who Stayed

Ulerion remained with Althair. He copied damaged notes, retraced safer journeys,
and learned which of his father's silences indicated shame rather than secrecy.
After Althair's death, Ulerion carried pieces of the archive toward Mosswood,
hoping to identify recurring attacks along the forest road.

He was murdered before reaching the village.

His body and a letter from Alerion are the first clear links in the Ashenport
Road. His satchel was opened, but nothing obvious was stolen. A single page had
been turned upside down and returned to its place.

This detail becomes important much later, which is why most official summaries
omit it.

## The Three Folk Shapes of a Darkling

Darkling is a condition of the soul, not a stable species. The Prisoner's Touch
alters people and creatures according to what they were and what their corruption
can use. Along the Ashenport Road, common speech groups sightings into three
shapes.

- A **shadowman** or **darkwalker** is a person made indistinct: smoke-edged,
  oil-dark, and difficult to remember accurately. The name describes appearance,
  not rank.
- A **souleater** is anything that hollows identity through feeding, mimicry, or
  enforced shared thought. Survivors report missing names before missing blood.
- A **behemoth** is a great creature whose existing strength has become a vessel
  for the Touch. The Troll King of Graven Hollow is the best-known local case.

Scholars object that this taxonomy confuses symptom, method, and body.

People on threatened roads continue finding it useful after the scholars leave.

## The Five Movements of the Inheritance

_The remainder of this document contains campaign secrets._

The Ashenport Road is structured as five movements. They describe the widening
of knowledge, not mandatory levels or an unalterable sequence of errands.

## First Movement: Smoke Under the Floor

### The Mosswood Border Village

The road begins in a small settlement under Mosswood protection, southwest of
the Federation's deeper councils. It is a practical place of mushroom sheds,
sap beer, militia drills, and arguments over how much forest may be cleared
before a clearing becomes policy.

Its elder receives Alerion's letter for Ulerion. Its resident wizard controls an
old gate ward and knows more about unusual fungi than manners. Neither exists to
teach metaphysical lessons to conveniently new souls. They are people managing a
frightened settlement with inadequate information.

When Ulerion is found dead beyond the safe path, the elder sends the letter to
Alerion with whoever discovered him. The wizard opens the ward only after the
traveler helps restore warning mushrooms harvested from the southern wood. The
exchange is a local necessity, not a universal law that nothing in Lumia is
free.

### Gahrn's Camp

East of Ashenport, the half-orc veteran **Gahrn** keeps a camp beyond the wall.
Alerion asked his sons to observe a training estate owned by Lady Raven. The
younger, Rusk, entered and did not return. The elder, **Yahg**, has remained
outside, trapped between fear and the certainty that courage ought to look like
entering alone.

Finding Yahg matters more than killing whatever patrols the path. His testimony
places Ulerion near the same estate days before his death.

### The Wizard Training Mansion

Lady Raven's estate advertises instruction in summoning and defensive magic. Its
students are real. So are the locked lower rooms.

Failed transformations have left several children and apprentices altered by
magic that continues revising their bodies. The old quest account instructed
intruders to kill them as an act of mercy. That instruction is not canon.
Some victims can be stabilized, some can be moved, some ask for an ending, and
some are too dangerous to approach without causing further harm. The ethical
weight belongs to the people in the room, not to a blanket objective written
elsewhere.

**Veralis Sorn**, a young mage working inside the estate, has preserved evidence
and hidden survivors. Behind a torch-braced panel lies a chamber the household
calls the Void: not the cosmic void, but a folded laboratory where Lady Raven
has been studying identity removal.

Lady Raven is Darkling-touched. Whether she entered the Touch willingly or
believed she could study it without being studied in return remains unresolved.
Her defeat exposes the first repeated sign in Althair's notebooks: a circle of
five empty spaces around a broken center.

It also reveals Rusk's amulet, placed carefully beside a bed no one has slept in.

## Second Movement: Fire in the Valley

### Graven Hollow

Graven Hollow sits in a defensible valley beyond the eastern routes. For
generations, its people maintained an uneasy peace with troll communities in the
nearby caves. The recent violence is therefore not explained by species or
ancient feud. Something changed.

Alerion's contact **Stohana** tracks raids through her general store, where
damaged tools and unusual purchases reveal more than heroic reports. Trolls have
grown stronger, more coordinated, and terrified of their own king.

### Tahgoth and the Weapon of Flame

An elderly ritualist known only as **the Old Man** preserves a valley legend:
the Behemoth can be wounded by a weapon carrying tree, oath, and daylight.

The components are:

- a fallen branch willingly released by the Great Oak, not timber cut by force;
- the broken blade of **Tahgoth**, Graven Hollow's most celebrated defender,
  whose ghost appears near the graveyard when asked a question rather than given
  an order;
- and a coal kept through sunrise by someone defending the valley for no promised
  reward.

The local smith can join them into **Flamewake**. Its exact form follows the
bearer: sword, spear, axe, or another honest weapon. The fire harms the Behemoth
because it preserves three freely given purposes in one form, not because trolls
possess a universal vulnerability to legendary steel.

### Blindbreak Rest

The Behemoth's throne is guarded by a ward whose missing counterpart lies at
**Blindbreak Rest**, once a Luminari contemplation chamber and now a Darkling
nest.

At its center hangs the **Orb of the Open Hand**. The orb does not blast enemies
or declare its bearer chosen. It makes concealed intent briefly visible as
pressure and color. In the old sanctuary it allowed disputants to see how much
of an argument was fear.

Used against the Troll King, it reveals a second presence knotted around the
creature's will. Flamewake can cut the knot. Killing the host without perceiving
it merely allows the presence to seek another body.

The valley can therefore be saved without making every troll an enemy and every
victory a corpse count.

## Third Movement: The Girl, the Mosaic, and the Missing Center

### The Dollhouse

Between old mining roads stands a village that repeats one afternoon. Doors
open onto rooms too small for the buildings containing them. Adults speak with
the certainty of dolls placed in a scene. At each sunset, a child can be heard
asking someone outside the village to come home.

The place is bound to the memory of a murdered girl whose name varies according
to the room in which it is spoken. An artifact rests within the repetition, but
the girl is not a lock to be solved on the way to it.

The inheritance advances only when the visitors learn what happened, return
choice to the memory caught there, and accept that freeing her may allow the
artifact to vanish. If they value the object more than the child, the Dollhouse
resets with them inside it.

The surviving object is a small pane called the **Mercy Glass**. It shows the
viewer the consequence they have been editing out of their own account.

### The Mosaic Cave

Althair's cave lies beyond routes that change after heavy rain. Alerion owns a
**wayfinder** from his father's collection: a palm-sized golden disc that opens
without visible seam, suspending a crystal needle above its lower half. The
needle points not north but toward the destination most strongly held in the
bearer's unspoken purpose.

This makes it useful and profoundly unsafe.

The cave contains a mosaic of Luminari figures surrounding a dark center. Later
hands cut five empty sockets into the design. A bloodstone traditionally
associated with images of Serane fits a separate hollow near the edge. When
light passes through Mercy Glass and the bloodstone together, the mosaic shows
not a map but a sequence:

1. a sphere made from many curved pieces;
2. the sphere broken by mortal hands;
3. pieces dispersed along roads of light;
4. a shadow gathering around the absence;
5. one piece turned upside down before being hidden.

The final image matches the page in Ulerion's satchel.

Veralis joins the investigation here. Her role is not to wait beside a wall and
assign extermination. She can read the mansion's ritual marks, argue with
Alerion's interpretation, and recognize that the five sockets were added long
after the original mosaic.

No vision at this site establishes that Serane appears in person or grants a
boon. She may communicate through the bloodstone. A Darkling may imitate her.
The difference should matter.

## Fourth Movement: The Keep and the Theft

### Meelka's Warning

**Meelka the Crone**, an old colleague of Althair's, locates a sealed laboratory
now called the Ruined Keep. The oldest notes say an aspiring Darkling caused the
catastrophe. Gnar families have lived in and guarded the chambers for generations.

Earlier accounts treat the Gnar as obstacles and instruct adventurers to kill
their guards, shaman, king, and queen in order to loot a chest. That account is
not canon.

The Gnar are custodians with law, memory, and reasons to distrust every visitor
who calls their inheritance an abandoned dungeon. Their shaman **Dalmada** holds
the route into the royal vault. Access can be earned through treaty, proof that
the old laboratory is waking, or help against corrupted mechanisms. Violence
may force the way. It also turns a possible alliance into another theft dressed
as exploration.

### The Runic Gauntlet

The vault contains the **Runic Gauntlet**, a pre-war control vessel that can
separate a joined magical lattice without shattering the material. Althair
believed it could safely dismantle the curved Arcanite pieces he had found.

Before an agreement is completed, **Swinton Goldwater** steals the gauntlet.
Swinton is a gnome antiquities thief with excellent timing, adhesive compounds,
and a conviction that ancient objects become morally unowned after enough
centuries.

His brother **Fenton**, an Ashenport tinker, once built the devices Swinton used
for legitimate salvage. Fenton stopped when a woman known only as **the Pale
Patron** began buying whatever Swinton retrieved from Darkling sites. He can
repair the failed transit device left at the keep and open a road to Swinton's
hideout.

Recovering the gauntlet does not decide who owns it. The Gnar claim custody.
Fenton claims responsibility for what his craft enabled. Alerion claims need.
The gauntlet itself closes whenever anyone calls possession simple.

## Fifth Movement: Roads of Light

### The Network of Agents

Alerion's network uses Swiftpaths because messages carried by ordinary road have
been intercepted and subtly corrected. The agents include bowyers, gemcutters,
innkeepers, clerics, harbor readers, and scholars across the Homelands. Their
names and routes may vary with the living campaign. They are not disposable
stations handing identical notes to whoever arrives.

Each preserves a different kind of evidence:

- a rubbing of one curved Arcanite piece;
- a report of a Swiftpath answering an unintended destination;
- a song whose missing harmony matches the Mosaic Cave;
- an inventory showing Althair purchased, then returned, a fragment decades
  earlier;
- and testimony that Ulerion was followed by someone wearing Alerion's face.

The route makes the Swiftpaths part of the mystery without reducing them to a
sequence of errands. Their approved nature and dangers remain those established
in `canon/locations/the_swiftpaths.md`.

### Ghalaerd Mossmere

The northern scholar **Ghalaerd Mossmere** compares the collected evidence. He
concludes that Althair's spherical lattice was an instrument for testing
metaphysical strain. It may have measured one or more Locks. It may have helped
maintain local expressions of them. It may have been built by later mortals who
mistook a model for the thing modeled.

He does **not** locate the Prison.

He does **not** send anyone through the Ethereal Plane, Abyss, or Avernus to copy
an inscription from its seal.

Those claims entered a late quest transcript without support from the approved
cosmology. No canon establishes the Prison in Avernus or any other mapped plane.

### The Broken-Key Hypothesis

Althair called the spherical lattice a **key**. The word survives in Alerion's
notes and in Darkling attempts to acquire the pieces.

The term is dangerously convenient.

The canonical Five Locks are Memory, Identity, Time, Space, and Will. They are
metaphysical anchors, not five dungeon doors. A material device might resonate
with a Lock, diagnose it, brace a local wound, or exploit a correspondence. It
does not thereby become the Lock or prove that opening a mechanism can release
the Prisoner.

The pieces are worth protecting precisely because no one knows which verb is
correct.

## The Places of the Road

### The Arcanite Workings

Darkling cells have reopened small mines and scavenging pits along the regional
network. Their purpose is not simply to gather wealth. They seek pre-cut lattice
pieces among ordinary crystal and deliberately encourage rivals to overmine the
rest.

The popular four-stage account—rats, changed miners, golems, cult leader—is a
builder's sketch, not fixed canon. A worthy Arcanite site should show the cost of
extraction, Crystal Dwarf claims, the prison crisis, labor conditions, and the
difference between a corrupted miner and a person harmed by exposure.

### The Underways

The word **Underworld** appears throughout the old route, sometimes meaning the
ordinary deep road network, sometimes a drow territory, and sometimes a plane of
the dead. These are not interchangeable.

The Mosaic Cave contains a glyph pronounced approximately _Ignitis_ that opens a
narrow way into Lumia's underground passages. What originally keyed the word is
unknown. It does not establish universal access to every underground or outer
realm.

### The Tutorial Grounds

Older plans described a metaphysical space where newly arrived souls learn to
move, fight, craft, and cast without consequence. No foundational canon requires
such a place. If retained in implementation, it should be understood as a
training enclosure maintained by a living institution—not proof that new
characters enter Lumia as disembodied souls or that consequences are suspended
inside the Loom.

## The Questions the Road Must Preserve

- Who killed Ulerion, and why was the page inverted rather than removed?
- Did Althair break the lattice to protect it, hide his mistake, or prevent it
  from completing a function?
- Is the Pale Patron a Darkling, a mortal collector, a divine servant, or a name
  used by several interests?
- Can the altered victims of Lady Raven be restored without imposing another
  unwanted form?
- What did the Gnar inherit besides custody?
- Does the wayfinder reveal desire, need, or the strongest will currently near
  the bearer?
- Why does Ghalaerd's sixth rubbing fit the sphere only when one of the other
  five pieces is absent?

An adventure may answer some of these questions. It should not answer all of
them with the same villain.

## Canon and Runtime Boundary

- The named people, places, relationships, and five-movement investigation are
  established adventure lore.
- Exact coordinates, room numbers, vnums, level bands, kill counts, quest tokens,
  command phrases, and transport implementation are not lore canon.
- Existing production zones may provide topology. Borrowed setting names must
  not become player-facing Lumia canon merely because a route once used them.
- Moral choices must remain choices. Mercy cannot be implemented as a mandatory
  extermination objective; custodians cannot be reduced to loot barriers by the
  canon text.
- The Road does not locate the Prison, materialize the Five Locks, or guarantee
  that a reconstructed lattice can open or close either.
