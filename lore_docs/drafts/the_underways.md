# The Underways

- **Lore status:** [DRAFT PROPOSAL]
- **Draft version:** underways-0.1.0
- **Proposed use:** Subterranean geography, settlement lore, travel traditions,
  faction hooks, and adaptation of imported underground zones
- **Spoiler level:** Player-safe through “Five Courtesies of the Road”;
  restricted thereafter

_Compiled by Hessa Marr, sometime air-counter of the Third Shore, from breath
ledgers, drowned maps, drow silk-charts, Ordrum load-songs, and the testimony of
three guides who agreed only that the fourth was lying._

> The surface believes a road is earth pressed flat beneath the foot.
>
> Below, a road is an agreement that the mountain will not close its hand today.

## Draft Status and Scope

This proposal gives one name—**the Underways**—to Lumia's connected physical
roads beneath the surface. It does not make every cave part of one realm, every
deep people subject to one law, or every darkness a door into something darker.

The Underways are stone, water, air, root, ruin, and labor. They are not a plane
of the dead. They are not the Otherworld. They are not the **Deep Road**, which
is the established magical path between a Titan and a mortal petitioner. A
traveler may encounter a thin place, a Titan shrine, or the unquiet dead below
the earth, just as a traveler may encounter them beneath an open sky. The
encounter does not turn geology into cosmology.

This draft proposes two great settlements as the principal powers of the
network:

- **Ordrum**, the Hammered City, a deep-dwarf metropolis built around a
  geothermal chasm and the civic burdens of breath, water, and weight.
- **Nharavel**, the City of Seven Silences, a drow metropolis spread through
  seven immense caverns behind a single public approach.

Both names and identities are original to this proposal. Imported labels from
the runtime topology are not adopted as Lumia lore.

## The Country Beneath

There is no single ceiling over the Underways.

In one reach, the stone hangs an arm's length above a crawl worn smooth by ten
thousand knees. In another, clouds form beneath a cavern roof no lamp can find.
There are rivers that have never seen rain, forests fed by the dreams of roots,
and abandoned stairs whose makers expected gravity to approach from another
direction.

Some passages were opened by water. Some were cut by miners, armies, pilgrims,
or creatures whose tools left no marks a mortal mason recognizes. The root-cities
of Quechian descend into warm caverns. The Crystal Reaches spread through vast
networks around Nagburim. Trelux tradition speaks, when it speaks at all, of
Deep Gardens older than the first mortal road.

These places touch the Underways without becoming interchangeable with them.
A road may reach a border. It does not thereby own the country beyond it.

No trustworthy chronicle names a single maker. Dwarven accounts say the mountain
first opened paths in order to breathe. Drow temple histories say darkness made
rooms so secrecy would have somewhere to live. Surface colleges attribute every
wall too smooth for their tools to the Luminari, which is an efficient way to
turn ignorance into a grant proposal.

The records are unclear.

The wear on the thresholds is not.

## How a Cave Becomes a Road

A hollow in the stone is merely space. An Underway begins when people accept
responsibility for passage through it.

Someone braces the roof. Someone tests the air. Someone marks the clean water
and carries warning to the next settlement when the water ceases to be clean.
Someone remembers which bridge sings before it breaks. If those duties end, the
road ends first. The tunnel may remain open for another thousand years, but it
has become wilderness again.

Underway surveyors therefore measure three things before distance.

### Breath

Air is the first road and the only one every traveler must use.

Guides read a flame's lean, the chill against a wet thumb, and the taste left by
stone dust on the back of the tongue. Ventilation shafts are guarded more
carefully than treasure rooms. Closing a gate without accounting for the air
behind it is treated in most settlements as attempted murder, even when no one
dies quickly enough to make the accusation convenient.

### Echo

Sound reveals width, water, movement, and lies.

A careful knock can find a side passage beyond sight. A careless song can announce
a caravan to listeners three caverns away. Ordrum maps are struck on plates and
memorized as rhythm. Nharaveli routes are embroidered in raised silk so they can
be read without light or noise. Hushwater pilots tap the hull and listen to what
the unseen shore returns.

An echo is never considered empty. It has touched everything between its leaving
and its return.

### Weight

Every road is carrying something.

The burden may be a bridge, a city, a lake pressing behind a mineral wall, or a
history no one has dared remove from its shelf. Underway law treats load as a
civic fact. A merchant who overloads a span has endangered strangers. A ruler
who adds another palace beneath a failing roof has done the same thing with
better masonry.

This principle has not prevented either crime.

It has merely given both a name.

### The Western Descent

**Frostmouth** is the best-attested western threshold. It lies beneath the
frostbound heights associated with West Ubdina's Frozen Castle, though no
approved account proves the castle was built to guard it. Meltwater moves
through the threshold in spring, coating the first stairs in clear ice and
awakening bells frozen into the wall.

Beyond it, the **Long Descent** divides. One road reaches Ordrum. The other enters
the **Switchmaze**, a defensive knot of gates, false inclines, quarry cuts, and
bridges that can be turned to face the wall. The maze changes because people
change it. Claims that the stone rearranges itself are usually told by guides
explaining a late arrival.

Usually.

Four outer branches leave the Switchmaze:

- the **Still Rooms**, where thought seems to arrive before speech and visitors
  are advised to keep one memory written outside their own minds;
- the **Ember Galleries**, a chain of heat-cut halls leading to the Furnace
  Keep and the giant smith-clans who dispute every smaller people's definition
  of ownership;
- the **Nameless Shrine**, whose altar has been scraped clean so many times that
  the absence itself has acquired worshippers;
- and the **Folded Court**, a ruined audience hall where distance occasionally
  behaves like rank and forces the humble to walk farther than the proud.

The Folded Court may be a thin place against the Feyveil. It may be damaged
architecture. No responsible guide sells certainty with the ticket.

### The Middle Roads

The **Common Vaults** are broad, inhabited caverns whose name describes law
rather than ownership. Ordrum, Nharavel, the Third Shore, and several smaller
communities recognize the vaults as passage that no single power may permanently
seal. This agreement has been broken seven recorded times and repaired eight,
which Underway jurists cite as proof that law is stronger than arithmetic.

The **Reed Caves** descend from the southern vaults into warm shallows inhabited
by fishers, water-growers, and families adapted to lives spent half in water.
Surface visitors have repeatedly mistaken difference for primitiveness. The
Reed councils answer by charging surface visitors twice for boats.

North and east of the vaults lie the **Deep Reaches**, old pressure caverns
connecting the middle roads to the Veiled March and the Glassroot Reach. Their
air can turn foul without warning. Caravans travel there with caged moss rather
than birds: the moss pales before poison can silence anything capable of fear.

### Hushwater

At the center of the known Underways lies **Hushwater**, a freshwater expanse
wide enough that a vessel may lose every wall and still remain beneath the
world.

Its surface is black because most light is swallowed before reaching it, not
because the water is corrupt. Pale mineral rafts drift across it. Blind silver
fish rise when boats sing low notes. In the deepest season, water falls from the
roof as rain, having completed a weather cycle entirely inside stone.

Hushwater joins Ordrum, the Common Vaults, the Sable Ward, the Siltcoil, and
Lantern Reach. It is road, granary, border, and threat. Every city depends upon
it. None controls more than a shore.

The **Siltcoil** is a living route through soft eastern stone. Vast pale burrowers
move with the mineral season, opening passages and closing others behind them.
The people who follow do not worship the creatures. They show the professional
courtesy owed to anything whose digestion determines the map.

**Lantern Reach** narrows westward into the Toll Tunnels and the **Third Shore**,
a market that possesses no shore except by legal insistence. The name began as a
joke between Hushwater's two great powers. It became useful, then profitable,
then sacred in the manner of many jokes that acquire warehouses.

### The Eastern Marches

The **Sable Ward** is a fortified shore and the only public land approach to
Nharavel. Its outer road returns to Hushwater. Its inner road enters the city.
A third climbs into the **Veiled March**, where watch-posts guard the Mosaic
Descent, the Deep Reaches, and two less orderly territories.

The **Echo Burrows** are inhabited excavation villages whose oldest residents
navigate through vibration more readily than sight. They trade stone salts,
ceramic resonators, and news carried through walls. The **Broken Web** is a
fractured maze occupied by exiles, dissident drow households, smugglers, and
people who prefer a dangerous road to a safe gate that remembers their debt.

The **Mosaic Descent** rises toward the established Mosaic Cave. Its upper way
still answers to the glyph pronounced approximately _Ignitis_. This draft does
not explain who keyed the glyph, make it a universal passphrase, or turn the
Mosaic Cave into a route to every place beneath Lumia.

### The Glassroot Reach

The **Glassroot Reach** begins where ordinary stone grows bright-veined and
sound travels farther than the hand that made it. It leads from the Deep Reaches
toward crystalline caverns and, beyond them, the sealed **Ash Vault**.

Crystal Dwarf surveyors reject most surface maps of this region. Some routes
approach the Crystal Reaches; some end at gates whose keepers acknowledge no
outside jurisdiction; some may be abandoned Arcanite workings that should
remain abandoned. No route shown here grants access to Nagburim Prime.

The Ash Vault's builders are unknown. Its black doors are warm even when the
surrounding crystal is cold. One account calls it a fortress. Another calls it
an apology. The doors have not clarified the distinction.

## The Two Great Settlements

Ordrum and Nharavel are called the Two Pillars by merchants who need both cities
to remain standing. Their own diplomats dislike the phrase. Ordrum objects that
a pillar bears weight while Nharavel merely hangs from it. Nharavel replies that
a pillar is what one builds when architecture has no imagination.

They have fought by toll, embargo, proxy, marriage, sabotage, hymn, silence,
and once by exchanging increasingly impractical ceremonial gates.

They have not fought a full war in living memory.

Hushwater is the reason.

## Ordrum, the Hammered City

> Stone bears the mountain.
>
> We bear what stone forgets.

Ordrum descends in twelve inhabited shelves around a geothermal cleft called
**the Bellows**. Bridges cross the opening at different heights. Forges cling
to its warm walls. Cisterns occupy the cool stone beyond, and the upper vaults
carry air down through shafts wide enough to swallow surface towers.

The city is enormous without being broad. From the Frost Gate to the Low Cistern,
a quick walker spends most of a day descending and arrives with soot in places
clothing was meant to prevent. Whole districts hang beneath other districts.
Children learn early that a neighbor may live across the street, above the
ceiling, or two bridges down and one echo left.

No agreed translation of _Ordrum_ survives. Temple records render it as “held
thunder.” Forge guilds prefer “the work that answers.” Surface merchants hear
the word _drum_ and assume it describes the city's constant hammering.

Ordrum citizens consider this charming in the way one considers a dog charming
for misunderstanding a mirror.

### The Dwarves Who Remained Flesh

Ordrum's oldest households descend from dwarven miners, refugees, engineers,
and defeated armies that remained below during the Last War. Unlike the clan
sealed among Nagburim's Arcanite fields, they lived among iron, salt, basalt,
and heat. They did not become crystal. They became excellent at remaining
alive while everything they built attempted to become a tomb.

They call themselves simply dwarves. Surface traders often say **deep dwarves**
when distinction is useful. The city also holds human vent-keepers, gnome pump
wrights, half-orc caravan guards, genasi heat-workers, and families too mixed
or too old to fit a surface census.

Ordrum is not a racial enclosure. It is a civic burden with addresses.

Citizenship is earned by accepting responsibility for one piece of shared
survival: a span, pump, vent, cistern, warning bell, fungus bed, or burial road.
Wealth may purchase labor. It cannot purchase the name of the burden. A noble
whose vent poisons a lower ward is named beside every death until the vent is
made safe and the final family accepts restitution.

The naming is considered punishment.

The restitution is considered the beginning.

### The Three Burdens

Ordrum is governed through three offices rather than one throne:

- The **Bearer of Breath** oversees vents, fires, smoke law, and evacuation.
- The **Bearer of Water** oversees cisterns, Hushwater works, waste, and flood
  gates.
- The **Bearer of Weight** oversees bridges, excavation, load, and the city's
  deep foundations.

Each Bearer is selected by the guilds and burden wards most affected by the
office. Each may halt a project that endangers their charge. Two may remove the
third. All three must sleep one month each year in the lowest district governed
by their decisions.

The law began after six upper families declared the air crisis solved while
three lower wards continued burying children.

Ordrum remembers this as constitutional reform.

The lower wards remember names.

### Work, Worship, and the Cooling Table

Ordrum exports pressure-forged steel, pumps, chainways, ceramic pipe, load-stone,
salt, and tools designed to be repaired by hands that did not make them. Its
greatest engineers pride themselves on accomplishing with leverage and patience
what surface mages accomplish with Arcanite.

This pride is not purity. Ordrum uses Arcanite in medicine, resonance testing,
and a few old civic works. It restricts new structural dependence upon the
crystal because depletion threatens the Prison and because no city should build
its breath around a substance another people require in order to have children.
Smugglers regard this principle as an invitation with inconvenient wording.

Borhild, Skarn, Duerak, Maela, Vangar, and Orith all receive worship in Ordrum.
No god owns the city. The largest sanctuary is the **Cooling Table**, a black
iron slab where guild disputes are argued while a newly forged bar loses its
heat. No verdict may be spoken until every party can place a bare hand upon the
metal.

The custom was intended to cool anger.

It has also produced remarkably long arguments and exceptionally well-tempered
bars.

### The Weight-Debt

Ordrum's virtue is responsibility. Its favorite sin is turning responsibility
into ownership.

Families unable to repair an inherited burden may enter **weight-debt**, binding
their labor to the guild that assumes it. The contract was designed to prevent
bridges and pumps from failing when a household failed. In the lower shelves it
has become hereditary in everything but official language.

The Unburdening movement demands fixed terms, civic assumption of essential
works, and an end to children inheriting contracts they never struck. Several
guilds agree in public and purchase additional debt in private.

Meanwhile, the Bellows is cooling.

No one knows why.

## Nharavel, the City of Seven Silences

> In darkness, a secret is a wall.
>
> In Nharavel, walls have learned to listen.

Nharavel occupies seven linked caverns east of the Sable Ward. Each is so vast
that its farther architecture fades into its own weather. Towers descend from
the roof like black teeth. Bridges cross one another at angles that make surface
masons accuse the city of cheating. Curtains of cultivated silk divide markets,
homes, shrines, gardens, courts, and those private wars respectable Houses call
genealogy.

The city is not silent. It contains mills, waterwheels, foot traffic, arguments,
children, bells, insects, prayer, and the constant whisper of silk under tension.
Its Seven Silences are legal conditions, one assigned to each cavern:

1. **Silence of the Blade:** no weapon may be drawn without naming the life it
   intends to alter.
2. **Silence of the Face:** masks are required in court so an argument cannot be
   judged by the rank of the mouth carrying it.
3. **Silence of the Door:** sanctuary cannot be violated by public authority
   without three unrelated witnesses.
4. **Silence of the Ledger:** a debt may be hidden from gossip, never from the
   debtor.
5. **Silence of the Web:** House business is protected until it crosses a public
   road.
6. **Silence of the Dead:** the final name of the deceased belongs to those who
   mourn them.
7. **Silence of the Unnamed:** those without House, guild, or recognized kin may
   speak before any titled petitioner.

Every Silence began as protection.

Every Silence has been used as a weapon.

### The Name Beneath the Name

Nharaveli scholars offer three translations for their city's name: “darkness
held open,” “the promise beneath the knife,” and “where seven echoes kneel.”
Temple etymologists insist all three are corruptions of a sacred phrase they
will not pronounce for outsiders.

An exile named Sira Venn once translated it as “home, despite everything.”

Her version is not taught.

It is the one most often carved beneath departure gates.

### The Veiled Table

Seven masked Speakers govern Nharavel at the **Veiled Table**. Four chairs belong
to old Matron Houses, two to craft confederacies, and one to the Unnamed Ward.
The mask belongs to the chair rather than the Speaker. While seated, a governor
has no personal name in law and cannot claim private insult as public injury.

This system prevents certain forms of tyranny with great elegance.

It enables others with equal elegance.

House **Vel'Sharen** holds one of the old chairs and maintains shrines, silk
gardens, and blood claims reaching far beyond the city. Its long pursuit of the
exile line that produced the captain of the Forgotten Tide is neither forgotten
nor universally supported.

House **Mal'Drakon** holds no permanent chair at present, though its traders,
informants, and marriage contracts sit at every side of the table. It manages
much of Nharavel's covert surface exchange and officially denies all commerce
with Inkwater.

The quality of Nharaveli lacquer found at Inkwater makes the denial a devotional
exercise rather than a factual claim.

### The Spider Queen and the Forty Names

The oldest priesthood in Nharavel serves the **Spider Queen**. Her temples teach
that she spun the first useful darkness and placed the drow within it as both
children and test. House rites disagree over whether the Seven Silences are
strands of her mercy or knots restraining her hunger.

The public divine concordance does not include the Spider Queen among its Forty
recognized names. This does not prove she is unreal. It proves that Lumia's
largest surface temples cannot trace her worship, manifestations, and
relationships with enough confidence to classify her.

Nharaveli theologians offer answers surface scholars dislike:

- she is a Woven God whose anchors were deliberately hidden;
- she is an Under-Shadow title shared by Velara, Zhaerin, or another power;
- she is an elder being that accepts worship without belonging to either divine
  order;
- or the priesthood itself has become the answering thing.

The temples agree on only one point: asking the Queen to settle the matter is
an invitation, not an experiment.

Nyxara, Umbros, Velara, Zhaerin, and Draven also receive open or concealed
devotion in the city. Faith follows House, craft, fear, gratitude, and private
experience. No honest account calls it singular.

### Silk, Ink, and Captive Labor

Nharavel exports cave-spider silk, mushroom lacquer, mineral inks that remain
legible in darkness, compact bows, medicines, poisons, and pilots trained to
navigate water without sun or stars. Its ceiling gardens hang in layered nets.
Its waterwheels turn sideways in falling streams. Its finest bridges are woven,
not built, and are replaced one strand at a time without ever being closed.

The beauty is real.

So is the bondage beneath it.

Captive labor and inherited debt remain legal in three Silences and concealed
behind household law in two more. The Unnamed Ward, several craft guilds, and a
movement called the **Cut Thread** work to buy, steal, litigate, or break people
free. Their opponents argue that House obligation is the structure holding the
city together.

Ordrum abolitionists answer that a bridge unable to stand without a person
chained beneath it deserves to fall.

Nharaveli reformers answer that it is easy to recommend collapse from the other
shore of Hushwater.

Neither answer feeds the person carrying the bridge today.

### The Seventh Silence Is Failing

For three generations, the Unnamed chair has allowed a petitioner without rank
to speak first at the Veiled Table. In recent months the chair's mask has begun
whispering the petition before its holder opens their mouth.

Some call this espionage.

Some call it prophecy.

The Spider Queen's eldest priests have called it “the return of proper order,”
which has frightened even several Houses that prefer proper order very much.

## The Hushwater Compact

Ordrum and Nharavel signed the first Hushwater Compact after a century in which
each city attempted to ruin the other's shipping and succeeded mainly in
starving smaller settlements.

The Compact has four surviving articles:

1. No power may poison, drain, dam, or magically redirect Hushwater without the
   consent of every inhabited shore affected.
2. Any vessel must aid another in sinking danger until passengers are safe,
   after which ordinary hostility may resume.
3. Air shafts, flood bells, and collapse warnings belong to the road rather than
   the city maintaining them.
4. No Arcanite working may alter the lake's level, current, or memory.

The Third Shore keeps the common copy. Ordrum keeps a version hammered in iron.
Nharavel keeps one embroidered in black silk. The Reed Caves preserve the
articles as a water rhythm struck against hollow gourds.

The wording differs.

The obligations do not.

Neither metropolis trusts the other. Both trust hunger, flood, and bad air to
punish a broken compact without regard for who was technically correct.

## Peoples Without a Single Banner

The Underways are not divided cleanly between two cities.

The Common Vaults contain mixed settlements older than several surface borders.
The Reed Caves follow water councils. Giant smith-clans of the Ember Galleries
recognize treaties only after testing whether the treaty can survive heat.
Exiles in the Broken Web form households by rescue rather than blood. The Third
Shore belongs to anyone whose stall remains open and whose fees are paid before
the lamps are lowered.

The Crystal Reaches are a sovereign underground power in their own right.
Nagburim's gates, Tal-speaking communities, caste disputes, Arcanite dependence,
and Harmonic Consensus remain as established in their own concordances. Ordrum
is not a lesser Nagburim, and Nharavel is not its shadow. All three powers share
stone. None shares a future willingly.

The Trelux Deep Gardens may touch the far Underways. Travelers report
bioluminescent wing patterns, hexagonal chambers, and crops that disappear when
described too confidently. These reports do not locate a Great Contemplation
Nest or end the Trelux Great Silence.

Other communities keep names outsiders mishear, shorten, or replace with
descriptions. This proposal declines to turn every unfamiliar people into a
monster category. A settlement is not made less political because its citizens
see without eyes, breathe water, share dreams, or consider individual ownership
an illness.

Difference is not an encounter type.

## Food, Light, and the Shape of a Day

Underway food begins with fungus, blind fish, mineral-fed moss, root sap, cave
insects, and whatever can survive transport without seeing the sky. Ordrum
smokes mushrooms over vent heat. Nharavel ferments pale caps in sealed silk.
The Reed Caves grow long watergrass in terraces beneath the surface. Wealthy
tables import fruit and grain, partly for taste and partly to display command
over distance.

Light is practical, ceremonial, and political. Ordrum favors hooded lamps,
forgeglow, and reflective ceramic. Nharavel uses cold fungus, mineral oil, and
silk screens that reveal a silhouette without surrendering a face. Crystal
light is tightly controlled wherever an Arcanite source is uncertain.

There is no sunrise below. Communities keep time by shift bells, meal cycles,
cistern measures, fungal bloom, temple observance, and news carried from the
surface. Hushwater has a season called **ceiling rain**. Ordrum counts the slow
cooling of deep stone. Nharavel's civic day begins whenever the Silence of the
Face lowers its court masks.

Travelers who insist upon asking whether it is morning are told yes until they
stop.

## Five Courtesies of the Road

No authority enforces these customs everywhere. Nearly everyone notices when
they are broken.

1. **Answer the warning knock.** A person may be hiding from an enemy and still
   deserve to know the roof is failing.
2. **Name the water honestly.** Clean, bitter, mineral, sacred, sick, and unknown
   are different conditions.
3. **Leave breath for the next traveler.** Do not close, burn, or foul a public
   vent without raising an alarm.
4. **State which law follows you.** A House writ, guild bond, surface charter,
   or no recognized protection changes what a guide risks by helping.
5. **Never call a cavern empty before listening.** The phrase has begun more
   wars than greed and ended fewer.

## Present Tensions

### The Falling Water

Hushwater has dropped by the height of a hand in three months. Ordrum blames
unlicensed pumps near the Veiled March. Nharavel blames new excavation beneath
the Bellows. Reed water-readers say the lake is not being drained.

They say it is going somewhere.

### The Cooling Bellows

Ordrum's geothermal cleft loses heat while distant vents exhale air warm enough
to blister moss. The Bearers disagree whether heat is moving, being drawn, or
being taught to answer another source. Pyrion's clergy have offered assistance.
Glacius's have offered patience. Neither offer reassures the lower shelves.

### The Whispering Chair

Nharavel's Unnamed chair continues speaking petitions before they are made.
Three petitioners have heard secrets they had not intended to reveal. A fourth
heard a childhood memory belonging to someone missing from the Sable Ward.

### The Glassroot Claim

Ordrum prospectors found a resonant vein near Glassroot and closed the work
without extraction. A Nagburim delegation arrived before the report reached the
surface. The Crystal Dwarves say the vein is not merely Arcanite.

They refuse to say what distinction comforts them.

### The Road That Forgot Its Guide

A caravan returned from the Still Rooms with every passenger, animal, and item
accounted for. Its route log is complete. Its pay ledger includes a guide.

No traveler remembers meeting her.

Her share of the food is gone.

### Marks at the Mosaic Descent

Fresh footprints have appeared above the highest dust line of the Mosaic
Descent. They begin at the closed threshold and lead downward. None lead up.

The _Ignitis_ glyph has not changed.

That is what worries the keepers.

## Restricted Concordance

If this proposal is approved, the following claims should become established:

- The **Underways** are Lumia's connected physical underground roads and
  inhabited caverns, not a plane or divine road.
- The known network has two major public surface thresholds associated with
  the Frozen Castle and Mosaic Cave, without claiming these are the only
  entrances.
- Hushwater is the central navigable freshwater basin of the mapped network.
- **Ordrum** and **Nharavel** are enormous, independent subterranean
  metropolises and the two principal powers around Hushwater.
- Ordrum is a predominantly deep-dwarf civic forge culture distinct from the
  Crystal Dwarves of Nagburim.
- Nharavel is a drow metropolis containing Houses Vel'Sharen and Mal'Drakon,
  multiple faiths, contested bondage, and the Seven Silences.
- The Common Vaults, Third Shore, and Hushwater Compact prevent either city
  from owning the entire network.

The following should remain unresolved:

- who or what first opened the oldest passages;
- the complete topology and true scale of the Underways;
- whether the Folded Court is an Otherworld thin place;
- the Spider Queen's metaphysical identity;
- the location of Trelux Deep Gardens and Great Contemplation Nests;
- what lies inside the Ash Vault;
- why Hushwater is falling and the Bellows cooling;
- and whether any Underway structure touches a Lock or the Prison.

No route in this proposal locates the Prison. No piece of underground
architecture is a physical Lock. Arcanite remains crystallized Aeonic residue
and part of the Prison wherever it is found.

## Canon and Implementation Boundary

This draft establishes no levels, encounter tables, travel times, room numbers,
resource yields, racial restrictions, or mandatory quest outcomes. Distances
and route conditions should serve lived geography rather than force a runtime
map to become literal scale.

Imported zone topology may guide implementation. Imported names do not become
player-facing truth by surviving in production data.

If Ordrum and Nharavel replace legacy settlement identities, builders should
adapt signs, inhabitants, factions, objects, worship, and surrounding routes
rather than changing only the entrance text. A name is the smallest part of a
city.

### Imported topology consulted

- lore_docs/drafts/diagram_exports/underdark-underworld.md

The imported diagram contributes twenty-seven topological nodes, twenty-eight
connections, two surface thresholds, a central water network, western and
eastern junction systems, a trade spur, a far crystal branch, and the roles of
two major settlements. Its borrowed location labels, implied cosmology, and
species assumptions are not promoted.

### Reconciliation decisions

- **Underways** is the proposed geographic term; **Deep Road** remains reserved
  for Titan-derived divine magic.
- Frozen Castle and Mosaic Cave are retained as surface anchors because both
  already occur in approved Lumia canon.
- Ordrum and Nharavel replace the two imported metropolis roles with original
  identities rather than disguising the old settings behind new spellings.
- Existing drow testimony—Houses Vel'Sharen and Mal'Drakon, the Spider Queen,
  underground navigation, mushroom lacquer, silk craft, bondage, exile, and
  Inkwater trade—is treated as foundation but expanded into a society with
  internal disagreement.
- Ordrum extends established organic dwarven presence and the Dwarven Hearth
  and Forge traditions without conflating deep dwarves with the transformed
  Crystal Dwarves.
- Nagburim, the Trelux, the Otherworld, the Five Locks, the Prison, and the
  divine roads retain their existing identities and mysteries.
