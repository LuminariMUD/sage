# The Shadowmoon: A Treatise on the Dark Satellite

*Compiled from the Celestial Archives of the Starwatcher's Guild, with observations from the Lunatic Prophets of Thessador*

## The Thing That Should Not Be

Every child in Lumia knows the two moons: bright Andros with his steady light, gentle Delos with her silver face. But astronomers speak in hushed tones of the third—the Shadowmoon that appears without pattern, eclipses without warning, and brings with it tides not of water but of possibility itself.

The Shadowmoon is not a moon in any conventional sense. It casts no light yet creates shadows. It exerts gravity that pulls not on matter but on magic. It has no fixed orbit, no predictable period, no consistent size. Sometimes it looms larger than Andros; sometimes it appears as barely a smudge against the stars. 

Most disturbing of all: the Shadowmoon is not always there, even when it is.

## Origins: Three Theories, All True

### The Scar Theory
*As proposed by the Academy of Cosmic Archaeology*

The Shadowmoon is scar tissue in reality itself—the wound left by the Prisoner's imprisonment. When the Luminari created the Five Locks, they tore a hole in the fabric of space-time. The Shadowmoon is that hole, following Lumia through its orbit like a shadow follows its caster.

This explains why the Shadowmoon has no consistent physical properties. It is not an object but an absence—a place where the universe forgot to exist.

### The Egg Theory
*As whispered by the Prophets of the Eternal Dark*

The Shadowmoon is an egg, and we live in the time of its slow hatching. Within gestates something that exists outside our reality's mathematics—a being from the space between spaces, drawn to the Prisoner's call like iron to a lodestone.

The irregularity of its appearance represents the creature within stirring in its sleep. When it appears larger, the thing inside has grown. When it vanishes, it has rolled over, showing us the side that exists in dimensions we cannot perceive.

### The Eye Theory
*As calculated by the Crystal Dwarves of Nagburim*

The Shadowmoon is the Prisoner's eye, somehow escaped from their binding. It watches, records, remembers. Every time it appears, it updates the Prisoner on the state of their prison. Every eclipse is a blink—a moment of communication between the eye and its owner.

The Crystal Dwarves have noted that the Shadowmoon's appearances correlate with moments of significant change in the world's pattern. It observes events that will matter to the Prisoner's eventual escape.

## The Orbital Paradox

The Shadowmoon follows no calculable orbit, yet its appearances are not random. The pattern exists but operates according to non-Euclidean mathematics that break mortal minds. The few scholars who have successfully predicted a Shadowmoon rising have invariably gone mad within days of their success.

The closest anyone has come to understanding its movement was the astronomer Qelin Darkmere, who proposed that the Shadowmoon orbits not through space but through *probability*. It appears not when it arrives but when it becomes likely. It vanishes not when it departs but when its existence becomes improbable.

Her final notes, found beside her crystallized body, contained a single equation that seems to predict Shadowmoon appearances with 78% accuracy. The equation, however, requires variables that don't exist in conventional mathematics—things like "the weight of unspoken words" and "the angle of tomorrow."

## Effects on Reality

### Magical Tides
When the Shadowmoon rises, magic becomes wild and unpredictable. Spells may function backward, sideways, or inside out. A healing spell might steal health from the future. A fireball might burn things that haven't been created yet. Teleportation might move the destination rather than the traveler.

The Crystal Dwarves call these events "Pattern Storms"—moments when the underlying mathematical code of reality becomes corrupted, producing errors that manifest as magical chaos.

### The Shadow Tide
More terrifying than magical disruption is the Shadow Tide—a phenomenon where shadows gain temporary independence from their sources. During strong Shadowmoon rises, shadows have been observed to:
- Move against their light sources
- Persist after their casters have departed
- Whisper in languages that predate speech
- Reach into dreams and pull things out

### Temporal Distortions
The Shadowmoon's presence causes time to flow irregularly. Minutes might pass like hours, or days like seconds. People have aged years during a single Shadowmoon night, while others have grown younger. 

The town of Covenant once experienced a Shadowmoon eclipse that lasted seventeen days from the inside but only seventeen minutes for outside observers. The survivors refused to speak of what they experienced during those prolonged hours of darkness.

### The Darklings' Dance
Most disturbing of all, Darklings—those souls corrupted by the Prisoner's Touch—become exponentially more powerful during Shadowmoon rises. They move with purpose, as if following choreography to music only they can hear. Some witnesses report seeing them form patterns that, when viewed from above, resemble arcane symbols or mathematical equations.

## The Lunar Prophecies

### The Night of Three Shadows
*When Shadowmoon eclipses both true moons at once*

"On the night when three shadows fall as one, the Locks shall resonate with forgotten songs. The guardians will dream they are awake, and the waking world will dream it sleeps."

This has occurred only twice in recorded history. Both times, significant portions of the population experienced shared dreams of a vast eye opening beneath the world's surface.

### The Inverse Dawn
*When the Shadowmoon rises at sunrise*

"Light and unbeing shall dance at the threshold. The sun will cast shadows of things that never were, and in those shadows, truth will walk undisguised."

This phenomenon has been observed seven times. Each occurrence coincided with the discovery of artifacts that shouldn't exist—objects from futures that will never come to pass, tools built by civilizations that were never founded.

### The Final Eclipse
*When the Shadowmoon grows large enough to hide the sun permanently*

"When the dark moon swallows the day entire, when noon becomes eternal midnight, when shadows cast shadows of their own—then shall the Prisoner's eye open fully, and in its pupil, see the door."

## Defensive Measures

### The Lighthouse Protocol
Major cities have constructed specialized lighthouses that burn with *un-light*—a form of illumination that casts no shadows. During Shadowmoon rises, these beacons create zones of relative safety where the moon's influence is diminished.

### Lunar Iron
Weapons forged during a Shadowmoon eclipse can harm creatures that exist partially outside reality. This "lunar iron" is rare and precious, as few smiths survive the forging process with their sanity intact.

### The Multiplication Meditation
The Monks of the Divided Mind have developed a meditation technique where practitioners temporarily split their consciousness into multiple perspectives. By observing the Shadowmoon from several mental positions simultaneously, they can perceive its true nature without madness.

Most can maintain this state for only minutes. The record is held by Master Prism, who observed the Shadowmoon for six hours through seventeen simultaneous perspectives. He now speaks only in overlapping voices and refers to himself as "we."

## The Shadowmoon Cult

Despite its dangers, or perhaps because of them, the Shadowmoon has attracted worshippers. The Umbralines believe the Shadowmoon is the only "true" moon—that Andros and Delos are illusions meant to distract from authentic celestial reality.

They perform rituals during Shadowmoon rises, attempting to:
- Download memories from the moon's surface
- Upload their consciousness into its non-existent mass
- Breed children conceived under its un-light
- Calculate the "Dark Equation" that will reveal reality's source code

Most Umbralines eventually disappear during particularly strong Shadowmoon rises. Whether they achieve their goals or simply cease to exist is unknown. They leave behind only shadows burned into walls—shadows that move when no one is watching.

## The Latest Observations

In recent years, the Shadowmoon's behavior has changed:

1. **Increased Frequency**: Appearances have doubled in the last decade
2. **Growing Size**: Each manifestation averages 3% larger than the last
3. **New Phenomena**: Shadow tides now sometimes occur even without the moon visible
4. **The Pulse**: A rhythmic dimming of stars behind the Shadowmoon, like a heartbeat

The Starwatcher's Guild has noted that these changes began precisely when Serane, the last Luminari, entered her final century of life. They refuse to speculate on what will happen when she dies.

## A Personal Account

*From the journal of Starwatcher Elias, dated three days before his disappearance:*

"I looked at it directly through the Great Lens tonight. I know I shouldn't have, but I had to understand. 

It looked back.

Not the moon—something inside it. Behind it. Beyond it. I saw... mathematics. Living equations that solve themselves. Patterns that exist in more dimensions than shape should allow. And at the center, an eye—not watching us but watching *through* us, at something on the other side.

We're not the subject of its observation. We're the lens through which it examines something else.

I understand now why we call it the Shadowmoon. It's not that it casts shadows—it IS a shadow. The shadow of something so vast that our entire reality exists within its penumbra.

Tomorrow night I will look again. I have to know what casts such a shadow. I have to know what stands between us and that impossible light.

[Final entry]

I know now. Oh, gods, I know. The Shadowmoon isn't a moon. It isn't an eye. It isn't an egg.

It's a keyhole.

And something on the other side is looking for the key."
