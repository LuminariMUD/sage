# The Sunkwater March and the Whitehook

- **Lore status:** [DRAFT PROPOSAL]
- **Draft version:** surface-settlements-0.1.0
- **Proposed use:** Geographic placement, settlement adaptation, regional hooks,
  and replacement identities for two imported surface-city zones
- **Spoiler level:** Player-safe

_Compiled by Merra Two-Reeds, coast clerk and sometime rope-reader, from Hiri
port ledgers, Oorpic storm knots, wilderness surveys, builder maps, and the
testimony of citizens who agreed that every map was wrong in a different way._

> A city does not belong where its old name remembers.
>
> It belongs where its roads arrive.

## Draft Status and Scope

This proposal places two imported city zones within Lumia's established
geography. It does not add a fourteenth Homeland, redraw a national border, or
promote an old setting under a lightly altered spelling.

It proposes:

- **the Sunkwater March**, a wet southeastern subregion of canonical **Hir**,
  with **Rootfast** as its principal settlement; and
- **the Whitehook**, the northwestern reach of canonical **Oorpii**, with
  **Koorvik**, the Measured Harbor, as its principal settlement.

Rootfast is the proposed replacement identity for the legacy Tugrahk Gol city
zone. Koorvik is the proposed replacement identity for the legacy Neverwinter
city zones. Both names and the local identities developed here are original to
this draft.

The word _region_ is geographic in this document. The Sunkwater March and the
Whitehook are subregions inside existing Homelands. They do not create new
character-creation values or new Heart Tongues. Rootfast is Hiri country.
Koorvik is Oorpic country.

## What the Map Establishes

The wilderness and transport records give unusually strong placements.

| Imported zone | Zone map coordinate | Canonical port anchor | Port coordinate | Draft conclusion |
| --- | ---: | --- | ---: | --- |
| Tugrahk Gol | `(-54, -320)` | Hir east seaport | `(-57, -330)` | A settlement in eastern Hir |
| Neverwinter | `(-593, 803)` | Oorpii northwest seaport | `(-597, 804)` | A settlement in northwestern Oorpii |

The first pair lies about ten map units apart. The second lies about four. On a
world map capable of misplacing a road by the width of a kingdom, these are not
close fits. They are the same places described by two different systems.

The terrain agrees. Tugrahk Gol's marker stands in a green coastal basin below
a long mountain spine. Its rooms describe swamp, dense forest, fog, tar pits,
and the rare patches of firm ground on which a road can survive. Its associated
mine lies immediately east at `(-29, -335)`, where the foothills approach the
coast.

Neverwinter's marker stands on the exposed western edge of a snow-covered
northern hook. The land narrows southeastward, while a wooded height rises in
the peninsula's interior. Its rooms describe a walled river city, northern
mountains, docks, warehouses, bridges, public institutions, and craftspeople.
Those elements fit a cold Oorpic harbor without requiring the borrowed setting
around them.

### Names That Are Not Neighbors

Several legacy labels create a false impression of local geography.

- The standalone Neverwinter Wood zone is at `(358, 601)`, nearly a thousand
  map units east of the city.
- Mount Hotenow is at `(-302, 579)`, hundreds of map units southeast.
- The zone called Neverwinter Catacombs is at `(60, 54)`, on a different
  portion of the world map.

None should be pulled into Koorvik merely because its imported text repeats the
same franchise name. Koorvik may have local woods, heights, and burial vaults;
the distant runtime zones require independent identities and later evaluation.

## The Sunkwater March

- **Proposed stable ID:** `region/hir/sunkwater-march`
- **Kind:** Forested coastal fen and mountain-foot march
- **Geographic parent:** Hir
- **Political sphere:** Claimed by the Empire of New Anteria through Hir
- **Heart Tongue:** Hiri
- **Principal settlement:** Rootfast

### Card Summary

The drowned edge of Hir, where willow roots hold roads above black water and a
promise is judged by what remains after the flood.

### The Country at the Last Bend

Hir's rivers do not end politely.

They divide. They turn back upon themselves. They enter stands of willow and
dark cypress, forget which channel was first, and arrive at the eastern sea as
a hundred brown mouths speaking over one another. The people of the basin call
this final confusion **the Sunkwater**. Imperial maps prefer _the Eastern
Drainage District_, a name no one has successfully shouted across a flood.

The Sunkwater March begins where Hir's low hills press against the southern
mountain spine. Rain descends from the heights, silt descends from the rivers,
and both are caught in a forest whose roots have learned to breathe through
water. Fog settles before dawn. Methane lamps tremble blue over the deeper
pools. In the dry months, islands of black soil emerge long enough to be
claimed, planted, disputed, and lost again.

The march is not empty wilderness. Reed villages stand on driven piles. Ferry
houses hang their doors high enough for a boat to pass beneath. Hunters know
which slicks mark tar and which mark something breathing. Miners take the stone
road east toward the amethyst foothills, while traders pole shallow craft toward
the Hir seaport and the wider world.

Every useful path is maintained by somebody. This is the first law of the
Sunkwater, though no tablet records it.

## Rootfast, the Held City

- **Proposed stable ID:** `settlement/rootfast`
- **Kind:** Hiri marsh city and half-troll cultural center
- **Geographic parent:** The Sunkwater March, Hir
- **Proposed legacy replacement:** Tugrahk Gol

### The Name on Firm Ground

Rootfast stands where two enormous willows grip a buried foundation and refuse
to release it. Beyond them, ground that appears solid may be water wearing a
thin brown mask. Within them, streets wind among living trunks, ancient stone,
raised mounds, open markets, and the clearings where the city gathers.

The Common name is a translation of a Hiri compound. Scholars disagree whether
it means _ground made fast by roots_, _people made fast by belonging_, or _the
root that held when promises did not_. Rootfast citizens generally answer that
all three meanings are useful and none will help pull a cart from the mud.

Trolls and half-trolls form the largest part of the city, but not its whole.
Hiri traders, ogrekin labor houses, human ferriers, Registered Dead surveyors,
and families with no interest in a stranger's census share its raised paths.
Rootfast is not a camp waiting to become civilized. It is a city whose
civilization happens to understand that a wall is less valuable than a levee
and a levee is less valuable than the people who repair it at midnight.

### The Gate Nobody Claims to Have Built

Rootfast's eastern gate is older than the city's accepted histories. Its metal
is dark beneath rust but remains stubbornly sound. Branches, flowers, and small
lights are worked across its face with a delicacy that has encouraged several
offensive theories about who could or could not have made it. The gate hangs
between the two oldest willows as though the trees grew expressly to carry it.

Inside the city lie the remains of a palace, a guildhall, a trophy chamber, and
an amphitheater. Below lie flooded corridors, a ruined laboratory, and an
observatory built where no ordinary sky has ever been visible. Rootfast's
people use what can be made safe and mark what cannot. They do not claim to
have raised the first walls.

They claim something harder to dispute: when the old builders vanished,
Rootfast's people kept the place alive.

New Anteria has produced inheritance tablets asserting that the ruins were an
office of Old Anteria. Rootfast has produced three centuries of repair ledgers,
burial names, flood marks, and children born beneath the gate. Hiri law respects
both records. Hiri politics has not yet discovered how to survive respecting
both at once.

### The Ground Ledger

Rootfast adapts Hiri Riverhand to civic survival. Before flood season, each
household's promised labor is written on an upper line: piles driven, channels
cleared, boats provided, stores shared. After the water falls, the lower line
records what was actually done.

The space between is called **the depth**.

A household with a wide depth is not fined automatically. Illness, broken
tools, and rescue work matter. It must, however, account for the water between
its words and deeds before neighbors whose homes depended upon both. The
largest public arguments in Rootfast are therefore held after the flood, when
everyone is tired enough to tell the truth and angry enough to make it useful.

The city's council is called the **Two Banks**. One bank speaks for households,
hunt companies, markets, and ferries. The other speaks for roads, waterways,
ruins, and the Registered Dead who still hold civic office. Neither may pass a
measure without recording who will perform the work. Rootfast considers an
unassigned law a kind of decorative lie.

### Houses, Hunts, and the Second Chair

Rootfast homes rise inside hollowed living trunks, on earthen mounds, and over
old stone rooms whose original purposes are no longer known. Many roofs are
hide or layered reed. Wealthier houses own no more floor than their neighbors;
they own drier floor.

The Hiri Second Chair is usually hung from a ceiling beam here. Leaving it on
the floor would invite rot, flood, or an inattentive child to sit in it. At an
important meal the chair is lowered, named, and raised again before anyone
eats. Outsiders sometimes laugh at the precaution. Rootfast hosts let them,
because a custom that cannot survive practicality is merely furniture.

Hunt companies supply meat, hides, medicines, and news from the reed country.
Their prestige comes from returning with everyone they led out, not from the
size of what they killed. A hunt leader who brings home a great beast and
abandons a companion receives no song. A leader who returns empty-handed with
the lost receives three.

### The Amethyst Foot

East of Rootfast, wet ground rises into cramped tunnels and violet caverns. The
legacy mine zone preserves a useful physical relationship: it lies close to the
city, beneath the mountain foot, and belongs to the same regional economy.

This draft calls the workings **the Rootfast Amethyst Delves**. The name is
provisional. Their amethyst is an ordinary precious mineral unless later lore
establishes a specific Arcanite connection; color alone does not make a crystal
divine.

Some shafts are new. Others enter cut chambers older than Rootfast's gate.
Miners tell of water that flows uphill, shadows caught inside unbroken crystal,
and a sealed workface that answers every hammer blow with one knock fewer than
it received. Mine officials classify all three reports as drink, echo, and an
excellent reason to charge hazard pay.

### Trade and Present Tensions

Rootfast exports amethyst, tar, cured hide, bitter medicines, willow fiber,
smoked fish, and guides who can distinguish a road from optimistic water. It
imports grain from Pesh, forged tools, lamp oil, salt, and stone suitable for
building above the flood line.

The old Swiftpath links remain useful implementation topology. Rootfast's road
to Pesh reinforces its place inside Hir; its farther connection toward Carstan
explains foreign tools and glass reaching the march without pretending the
portals measure ordinary distance.

Five troubles currently occupy the Two Banks:

1. The eastern mine has broken into a worked chamber beneath the water table.
2. The old observatory has begun showing stars during daylight.
3. An imperial surveyor has arrived with a valid deed to the ruined palace.
4. One of the gate willows is putting out silver leaves in winter.
5. Flood marks on the western road are rising although the river is falling.

### Media Anchors

Show a humid forest fen beneath distant mountains: immense willows, raised
roads, black water, reed boats, inhabited mounds, old stone beneath living
roots, and a diverse city centered on troll and half-troll life. Avoid a crude
monster camp, a generic green bog, or an uninhabited ruin.

## The Whitehook

- **Proposed stable ID:** `region/oorpii/whitehook`
- **Kind:** Snowbound basalt peninsula and storm-cut island reach
- **Geographic parent:** Oorpii
- **Political sphere:** Free Cities of Kohn through Oorpii
- **Heart Tongue:** Oorpic
- **Principal settlement:** Koorvik

### Card Summary

Oorpii's far white hook, where a warm river divides the snow and every public
clock reminds a storm leader when to surrender the keys.

### The Island That Becomes a Peninsula

From the sea, the Whitehook resembles a bent finger of snow pointing west. Its
outer shore is basalt, surf, and long beaches of pale storm-ground stone. Its
interior rises toward a dark wooded height where winter loses some of its grip.
A narrow neck trails southeast toward the rest of the northern landmass.

Mapmakers call it a peninsula. Oorpii calls it an island.

Both are correct often enough to cause deaths. Great storms drive water across
the low neck and cut the Whitehook free for days or months. In colder years,
sea ice binds it back to shores no summer road can reach. Oorpic pilots therefore
define an island as land whose safe approach must be renegotiated. Surveyors
have objected for generations and continued hiring Oorpic pilots.

The Whitehook is Oorpii's northwestern member and its broadest inhabited back.
Small cliff villages keep rope bridges, signal masts, and storm cellars along
the southern neck. Koorvik holds the western harbor where the warm river meets
the sea.

## Koorvik, the Measured Harbor

- **Proposed stable ID:** `settlement/koorvik`
- **Kind:** Oorpic river port and charter city
- **Geographic parent:** The Whitehook, Oorpii
- **Proposed legacy replacement:** Neverwinter

### A Name Made for Wind

**Koorvik** is pronounced with a long first vowel and a final consonant meant
to survive bad weather. Oorpic etymologists have offered several translations:
_kept channel_, _work at the hook_, _harbor that answers_, and one impolite
reading involving a frozen boot. The city's oldest rope-rune can be read from
either end and supports all four.

In Common, Koorvik is called **the Measured Harbor**.

The name refers to its craft, its river, and its politics. Koorvik makes clocks.
Koorvik is divided by water. Koorvik measures authority because its people know
that power, like a storm tide, does the greatest damage when no one marks how
high it has risen.

### The Thawrun

A broad river passes from the Whitehook's wooded interior through the center of
Koorvik and enters the western sea. It is called **the Thawrun** in trade speech.
Its Oorpic names change with season, current, and whether the speaker is above
or below the last safe bridge.

The Thawrun does not freeze as readily as the land around it. No accepted
account explains why. The wooded height may hide geothermal faults. Warm
northern currents may force salt water beneath the river's flow. Some point to
Oorpii's reports of architectural fragments arriving from a coast that should
not exist. Others point out that a mystery remains a mystery after a committee
gives it three names.

The warm water keeps Koorvik's harbor working through much of winter. It also
powers the city's mills, baths, sluices, and celebrated water clocks. North and
south Koorvik face one another across the current, joined by bridges whose
maintenance is both civic duty and competitive art.

### A City of Bridges and Work

Koorvik's walls enclose two dense halves. Broad streets run from northern and
southern gates toward the river. Warehouses and docks occupy the western water.
Workshops cluster along the central ways. A wooded residential rise overlooks
the southern bank, while public squares, gardens, militia yards, libraries,
inns, shrines, markets, and guild rooms fill the districts between wall and
bridge.

The source city's craft identity is worth preserving. Koorvik is known for
water clocks, storm lamps, cold-weather instruments, bridge fittings, musical
mechanisms, fine books, and ship work that can be repaired with gloved hands.
Its craftspeople say beauty is not the enemy of survival. Beauty is how one
persuades the next generation to maintain what survival required.

The city imports much of its timber and metal. It exports finished mechanisms,
charts, trained militia engineers, and ships small enough to thread Oorpii's
reefs. The great public library keeps books beside rope-rune copies of emergency
instructions, on the principle that knowledge unread in a gale was poorly
stored.

### The Returning Clock

Koorvik conducts ordinary government through elected quay councils, craft
delegates, bridge wards, and open harbor courts. During a declared storm, raid,
fire, or ice failure, these bodies place the storm keys in the hands of an
Oorpic Harbor-Mother, as elsewhere in the league.

Koorvik adds one safeguard of its own.

When the keys are granted, water is released into the **Returning Clock**, a
public mechanism taller than three people. The clock records the duration of
emergency rule. When the first calm week ends the office, the former
Harbor-Mother must climb the clock stair, open its return gate, and let the
collected water run into the Thawrun while every borrowed key hangs in public
view.

The ceremony is not an accusation. It is an ending made visible.

Three Harbor-Mothers have refused to climb. One was too injured. One was
arrested. Oorpic histories disagree about the third, except that the clock
continued filling after the sky became clear.

The large central keep should therefore be adapted as **Stormhall**, seat of
emergency coordination, militia stores, and the Returning Clock rather than a
hereditary royal castle. Wealthy houses and old families may retain influence,
but no imported monarchy is required.

### Stormhall, Knotspire, and the Undersluices

The existing city topology supports several distinct districts and attached
zones. Their proposed working identities are:

- **Stormhall** for the central castle complex;
- **Knotspire** for the tower and magical-guild complex;
- **the Undersluices** for the sewers and deeper civic channels; and
- **Hookrise** for the wooded height outside the northern wall.

These are adaptation handles, not additional canon claims. Builders should
preserve useful room relationships, services, bridges, and civic density while
replacing imported rulers, rival cities, streets, factions, gods, and proper
nouns with Lumia identities.

A recent attack described throughout the southern city remains a useful scar.
This draft treats it only as a western sea raid whose final authorship is
unsettled. It does not import Luskan or establish a replacement enemy merely to
keep every old sentence intact.

### Present Tensions

Five concerns currently trouble the quay councils:

1. The Thawrun's best clock loses one minute whenever warm debris enters the
   harbor.
2. A storm cut the Whitehook's neck and revealed foundations beneath the
   crossing.
3. One craft delegation wants the Returning Clock stopped during wartime.
4. The Knotspire has received whistle messages from an uninhabited western
   skerry.
5. The next Harbor-Mother election may be declared before the previous storm
   is formally judged over.

### Media Anchors

Show a working northern river port on a snowy basalt hook: warm mist over dark
water, crowded bridges, walls and docks, precise mechanisms, storm lamps, rope
signals, sturdy timber buildings, and a wooded interior height. Avoid a generic
snowbound fortress, a royal fantasy capital, or a forest city transplanted from
the distant standalone wood zone.

## Canon and Implementation Boundary

This draft does not alter the Thirteen Homelands, the Five Nations, Homeland
runtime values, Heart Tongues, Swiftpath mechanics, zone levels, encounters,
travel costs, room numbers, or map coordinates.

The local `Luminari-Source/lib/world` files are treated as a builder snapshot,
not proof that production contains identical room text. A room-by-room
adaptation should compare the active production zones before implementation;
the regional placements remain independently supported by the wilderness and
sea-port records.

It does not establish a universal origin, temperament, or culture for all
trolls or half-trolls. Rootfast describes one city within Hir. It does not make
ordinary amethyst into Arcanite, identify the builders of the old ruins, or
resolve the Empire's claim.

It does not make the Thawrun's warmth proof of a volcano, an Otherworld breach,
or the impossible coast named in Oorpic reports. Those are competing
explanations until later lore chooses otherwise.

If Rootfast and Koorvik replace the legacy settlement identities, builders
should adapt signs, inhabitants, organizations, worship, quests, objects, and
attached subzones rather than changing only room titles. A city is not made
original by its gate sign while every citizen remembers another world.

### Reconciliation decisions

- The Hir and Oorpii placements follow explicit sea-port anchors rather than
  resemblance, imported setting geography, or Swiftpath order.
- Rootfast preserves the swamp city, ancient gate, living trees, ruins,
  underground works, hunt culture, markets, and nearby amethyst mine while
  rejecting a one-note monster settlement.
- Koorvik preserves the divided river city, docks, bridges, walls, civic
  institutions, dense services, water clocks, craft identity, and recent raid
  damage while reconciling them with Oorpic government and culture.
- The distant wood, mountain, and catacomb zones remain independent because
  the wilderness map places them elsewhere.
- The Sunkwater March and Whitehook are subregions, not new Homelands.
