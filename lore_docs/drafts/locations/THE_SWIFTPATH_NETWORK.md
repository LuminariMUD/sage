# The Swiftpath Network: Roads Between the Moments

*Cartographer's Note: This map cannot be drawn. The paths exist not in space but in the spaces between spaces. What follows is an attempt to describe waters that flow uphill, roads that arrive before they depart, and doors that open onto themselves.*

## The Nature of Swiftpaths

Before the Last War, before the Luminari walked among mortals, before even the Prisoner's binding, there existed the Builders—entities of pure intention who carved roads not through landscape but through reality's infrastructure. These paths remain, though their creators are long forgotten: corridors of crystallized possibility that allow near-instantaneous travel between distant points.

The Swiftpaths do not shorten distance; they step outside of it. A traveler entering a Swiftpath in Sanctus might emerge in Ashenport having aged not at all, while their shadow, traveling by conventional means, would require three weeks. Yet sometimes the reverse occurs—journeys of moments that age travelers by years, or travelers who arrive before they departed, meeting themselves coming the other way.

## The Known Paths

*Note: "Known" is a generous term. The paths shift, merge, and occasionally cease to exist when not observed.*

### The Primary Trinity

#### 1. The Merchant's Memory
- **Entry Points**: Every major market at the moment of its founding
- **Exit Points**: Any place where coin has changed hands in anger
- **Reliability**: High during trade seasons, dangerous during wars
- **Current Status**: Partially collapsed near New Anteria after the Crystal Dwarf Emergence

The Merchant's Memory remembers every transaction ever made along its length. Travelers report hearing the ghost-echoes of ancient bargains, feeling the weight of phantom coins in their pockets. Some emerge with goods they didn't purchase, paid for with currency that won't exist for another hundred years.

#### 2. The Warrior's Road
- **Entry Points**: Battlefields where honor overcame advantage
- **Exit Points**: Any place where a last stand was made
- **Reliability**: Stable but brutal—demands a toll in blood or courage
- **Current Status**: Increasingly active, pulsing with pre-war energy

Only those who have killed or been willing to die can walk the Warrior's Road. The path tests each traveler with visions of all the battles they never fought, all the deaths they never died. Warriors emerge either broken or unbreakable. There is no middle ground.

#### 3. The Lover's Lament
- **Entry Points**: Sites of legendary partings
- **Exit Points**: Places of unexpected reunion
- **Reliability**: Emotionally dependent—functions only for those truly seeking or fleeing love
- **Current Status**: Fragmenting; too many hearts break simultaneously in these dark days

The most unpredictable of the Trinity, this path responds not to intention but to emotional truth. Those who enter seeking profit find themselves walking forever. Those who enter with genuine longing might cross continents in a single heartbeat—or find themselves delivered not where they wanted to go, but where they needed to be.

### The Secondary Septet

#### The Scholars' Spiral
Links all libraries that have burned at least once. Travelers must carry knowledge they're willing to lose—the path demands payment in memories of things learned.

#### The Madman's Marathon  
Connects all asylums and sanctuaries. Only those who have touched madness can find the entrances. The path itself is said to be beautifully sane, a respite from the chaos of normal reality.

#### The Children's Chase
Appears only to those under thirteen years of age, connecting all the best hiding spots in the world. Adults who try to follow children into these paths find only solid walls.

#### The Prisoner's Parade
Links all jails and dungeons. Paradoxically, it can only be walked by the truly free—those without guilt, obligation, or attachment. Most who find it cannot enter it.

#### The Gardener's Gate
Connects places where things grow despite impossibility—flowers in the desert, trees on mountain peaks, hope in darkness. Requires the traveler to plant something at both ends of the journey.

#### The Musician's Measure
Links all places where a perfect note has been sung. The path itself resonates with harmony; travelers must maintain a constant tone or be ejected back to their starting point.

#### The Dying Light
Connects deathbeds to birthing chambers. Used primarily by midwives and priests who understand that endings and beginnings are the same door viewed from different sides.

### The Forbidden Three

#### The Traitor's Thread
- **Warning**: PATTERN HAZARD - DO NOT SEEK
- Links all sites of ultimate betrayal
- Those who walk it emerge changed, often unable to trust even themselves
- Rumored to lead to the Lost Seventh Knight Order's stronghold

#### The Suicide's Solution
- **Warning**: EXISTENTIAL HAZARD - SEALED BY DIVINE MANDATE
- Connected places where reality gave up
- The path itself is reportedly "tired" and tries to convince travelers to stop existing
- Last known use: 200 years ago, resulted in the un-happening of an entire bloodline

#### The Prisoner's Private Path
- **Warning**: ABSOLUTE INTERDICTION - KNOWLEDGE OF ENTRANCES PUNISHABLE BY MEMORY EXTRACTION
- Theorized to connect all five Lock locations
- May not actually exist; might be a trap laid by the Prisoner
- Those who claim to have found it describe only "a door that opens inward to everywhere"

## The Mathematics of Motion

The Crystal Dwarves have attempted to map the Swiftpaths using their probability matrices. Their findings are disturbing:

1. **The paths are conscious** - They remember their travelers and adjust routes based on past journeys
2. **They're degrading** - Each use slightly corrupts the path's mathematical foundation
3. **They're converging** - All paths are slowly bending toward a single point
4. **That point moves** - It corresponds to no fixed location but might be a when rather than a where

## Navigation Methods

### Traditional Wayfinding
Experienced Swiftpath navigators, called "Steppers," use mnemonic techniques:
- **Memory Anchors**: Holding a vivid memory of the destination
- **Emotional Resonance**: Feeling the "pull" of where you need to be
- **Shadow Watching**: Following shadows that fall toward Swiftpath entrances

### Arcane Techniques
- **Path Tokens**: Objects that have traveled the Swiftpaths before and remember the way
- **Probability Scrolls**: Mathematical formulas that calculate the likelihood of a path's existence
- **Blood Maps**: Maps drawn in the navigator's own blood that update as paths shift

### Dangerous Methods
- **Dream Walking**: Entering Swiftpaths while asleep (high risk of never waking)
- **Shadow Stepping**: Using one's shadow as a scout (risk of shadow independence)
- **Temporal Skipping**: Aging or youthing oneself to match the path's preferred timeline

## The Swiftpath Stations

At certain intersections where multiple paths cross, permanent structures have formed—waypoints that exist partially in normal space and partially in the between:

### Haven's Rest
A tavern that exists simultaneously in seven locations. The same bartender works all seven bars at once, conducting different conversations with the same mouth. The ale is always exactly as cold as you expect it to be.

### The Crossroads Market
A bazaar where merchants from different eras trade goods across time. Ancient coins buy tomorrow's bread. Yesterday's news is sold as prophecy. The market guards enforce only one rule: no one may buy their own past or sell their own future.

### The Terminal Garden
A peaceful grove where all paths pause. Travelers often rest here, sometimes for years, forgetting they had destinations. The garden's caretaker, an entity of uncertain nature, offers tea that tastes like home and advice that sounds like poetry.

## Current Anomalies

Recent reports from Steppers suggest the network is experiencing unprecedented disruptions:

### The Echo Effect
Travelers report meeting themselves on the paths—not past or future selves, but current selves from parallel journeys. These encounters usually end badly, as the universe dislikes redundancy.

### Path Bleeding
Swiftpaths are merging inappropriately. The Merchant's Memory now occasionally deposits traders in active battlefields. The Lover's Lament sometimes leads to libraries. The boundaries between paths are dissolving.

### The New Path
Steppers whisper of a new path that wasn't there before—a dark corridor that smells of arithmetic and sounds like the absence of sound. No one who has entered it has returned, but their shadows sometimes arrive at their intended destinations, acting out the journeys their owners will never complete.

### Temporal Storms
Sections of paths now experience "time weather"—storms where past and future collide. Travelers caught in these storms report experiencing their entire timeline simultaneously, living every moment of their existence at once until the storm passes.

## The Stepper's Guild Warnings

The Guild has issued the following advisories for current Swiftpath travel:

1. **Never travel during Shadowmoon rises** - The paths become "hungry" and may digest travelers
2. **Avoid paths that seem too eager** - If a path opens without being sought, do not enter
3. **Trust your shadow** - If your shadow refuses to enter a path, heed its wisdom
4. **Carry salt and silver** - Both substances help maintain coherence during path distortions
5. **Remember your name** - The paths sometimes try to convince travelers they are someone else

## The Ultimate Mystery

All Swiftpaths, regardless of origin or destination, pass through a single point—a non-place the Steppers call the Heart of Paths. Every traveler glimpses it, if only for an instant that lasts forever: a vast chamber containing a single door that has never been opened.

Above the door, written in languages that haven't been invented yet, is a simple message that everyone understands differently:

"This door opens only once. Choose your moment wisely."

Some believe this is the true prison of the Prisoner—not the Five Locks but this single, unopenable door. Others think it leads to the Builders' home, wherever or whenever that might be. The Crystal Dwarves claim it's a mathematical divide-by-zero error in reality's code, a place where the universe forgot to prevent impossibility.

Serane, the last Luminari, once said of it: "Every path leads there, but no path goes through. It is the destination we all share but none shall reach. When that door finally opens, all paths end, and the journey begins."

## A Stepper's Prayer

*Traditional, spoken before entering any Swiftpath:*

"I walk between the moments,  
I step beside the real,  
I am the space between the spaces,  
The pause between the wheel.  
  
Let my path be swift and certain,  
Let my shadow guide me true,  
Let me arrive before I've started,  
Let me be both one and two.  
  
And if I lose myself in walking,  
If I forget which way is home,  
Let my footsteps write the story  
Of the paths I'll never roam."
